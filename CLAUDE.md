# Game Feed: instructions for Claude

Nabeel is building a TikTok-style feed of mini-games: swipe up and a random game starts instantly. Games are built one at a time; he play-tests each as a hosted page, gives feedback, and approves it. Goal: 1,000 games. He is not technical; talk in plain words.

## At the start of every session
1. Read `games.json` (the official registry) and `LEDGER.md`. If he asks "where are we?", answer from these: number of games, each game with its status, whether it is on the app (`in_app`), and its play link, the feed app (`feed_app` in games.json: its page and its Android download), and what's next.
2. Check `planned` in `games.json`: the next game may already be designed, with its full spec in `specs/`. If he says "build the next game", build that one.
3. Read `LESSONS.md` and follow it: his taste, the rules every game must pass, how to test, and build notes for each existing game. Reuse code from the most similar existing game in `games/<id>/index.html`.
4. **Engagement factors are mandatory in every game, without being asked.** "Engagement factors" is Nabeel's name for the research in `PLAYBOOK.md`. Read it before designing or changing a game: the design principles (P1–P30), the **Satisfying Sounds** rules and sound kit (S1–S11), the checklist and the AVOID list. Pick 3–5 principles that suit the game (never all of them) and record them in the spec and in the game's `principles` field in `games.json`. The sound rules apply to every game. **Longevity (his notes, 18 Nov 2026: "remember what makes the games engaging"):** section H of the PLAYBOOK (L1-L10: immediate understanding, satisfying controls, fair challenge, a near goal on screen, visible improvement with the best always in sight, variety that changes decisions, meaningful choices, a retry within a second, something that is yours and stays, a ghost of your best) comes before any reward system; every spec answers each in a line.

## Choosing games: a balanced feed
His words (16 Nov 2026): "I like intense games, but for the feed, it's really not good to have all intense games. You should have a variety of games... I like a specific type, so you have to make that ratio balance." So his own ideas come first, and the games added beside them are picked for engagement, creativity and fun **and** to keep the feed varied: in each set of five, no more than two at intensity 4-5 (`feed.intensity`), at least one calm or light one (1-2), and a spread of genres and of one-thumb and two-thumb play. Check the whole registry's mix before proposing a set. **3D or not is my call, game by game** (his words, 17 Nov 2026, replacing his earlier "every game is 3D": "Any game you think would be better in 3D, do it in 3D... sometimes 3D doesn't fit what we want to do"; Hippodrome and Bandit were great without it). Use 3D where the world in front of the player makes the game (with weather and light that change); stay 2D where it reads better (cards, papers, a board). Say which in the spec and why. **He wants speed and the ball in his court** (17 Nov 2026): build the next set straight away without asking; he tests later and asks for changes. **Deeper games, easy controls** (his words, 17 Nov 2026, evening: "make the games more... like how Pirates is kind of complicated and has numerous things to it... pay very close attention to your research when it comes to the engagement factors and what makes the games addictive and what makes the games fun... many of the controls that you have done earlier... were not good. So you need to make the controls easy"): from set 4 every game has several parts and systems (phases, choices, kinds of enemy or item, power-ups, upgrades that carry from level to level, rank), built on the PLAYBOOK; and its controls are the simplest that work: taps, a swipe, one floating stick. No two-stick fiddling, no precise flick aims, no gestures to learn. **Every game is engaging and its controls are made for it** (his words, 17 Nov 2026: "all the games need to be engaging, so you need to be creative. You need to find a way to make the controls for each game that's appropriate"): design each game's controls from what the player is doing in that world (steer a drone with a tilt-like drag, cast a line with a flick, swing on a vine by timing a release), never one stock tap or joystick copied from game to game, and try them on a phone-sized screen before anything else is built. The ideas waiting are in `specs/ideas.md`. **Sets are five games:** each set has one or two of my own ideas and the rest from his list; his ideas that do not fit a set roll on to the next ones (his words, 16 Nov 2026), and wait in `planned` in `games.json`.

## Adding or changing a game
**Research before building, every time** (his words, 20 Nov 2026: "Before you make a game, I need you to do research... go online and play games similar to it. That way you'll be able to learn, and then you create a game, because you're just creating the same things over and over"). Before designing a game, look up the best games of its kind (reviews, guides, how their controls, items, music, feel and progression work on phones), write what you learned in the spec under **Research** (with the sources), and build from that: the genre's best controls on a phone, not a stock control copied from our last game. You cannot play other games yourself; say so honestly and study them instead.
**His rules for every game (20 Nov 2026, evening, with ideas 12-24; full text in `specs/his-list-2026-11-20.md`).** Phone first, with large touch controls that never cover the action; a short playable introduction, with something enjoyable to do within the first ten seconds; responsive controls, clear visual feedback, satisfying sound; mechanics introduced gradually and difficulty from more interesting challenges, not just more speed; progress saved automatically, pause anywhere; quick retries, and mistakes explained visually so the player sees how to improve; **separate Music, Sound effects and Vibration settings** (the kit's ⚙ Settings: music goes through `music(() => ...)`, everything else is a sound effect, `vibe()` obeys the switch and its strength); original places and characters, no real teams or brands. Each of his ideas 12-24 also comes with its own detailed directions: build from them point by point, and say in the spec where a direction is kept and how.
0. **Start every new game from the starter kit** (`kit/README.md`): `python3 tools/new_game.py <id> "<Name>" --prefix xx. ...` makes the folder, the registry entry and the design stub; the kit already has the instant start, level memory, pause menu, lessons, smoothness, juice and the robots. Write the game's rules, drawing and sounds over the example. **One command runs every check:** `python3 tools/check_game.py <id>`.
1. Put the engagement factors in from the first build (the chosen principles, the sound rules, juice, haptics, honest feedback, personal best, help after losses where it fits). Never ship a game without them.
2. Build the game as one self-contained `games/<id>/index.html` (canvas, inline JS/CSS, Google Fonts with fallbacks, synthesized sounds built from the PLAYBOOK sound kit). Give it its own `storage_prefix` for saves. Give it an **instant start** for the feed from the first build: `window.gameFeedStart('mini')` must have him playing within a second of tapping its card, with no menu, countdown or intro, at his own level, without changing who wins (LESSONS rule 7). **Tutorials are optional** (his words, 18 Nov 2026: "make tutorial optional... a clear button to say skip the tutorial and just start playing... there should always be a button... to do a tutorial round"): every lesson shows a **Skip tutorial** button (`#skipTut`; it skips all the lessons for good), and the home card and the pause menu always have a **Tutorial** button (`#tutBtn`, `#pTut`) that plays the lessons again on any level; the kit has it, and `python3 tools/test_tutorial.py <id>` proves it. Give it the **basic controls** from the first build too (LESSONS rules 4 and 11): it remembers the level he is on (a win moves it on at once; lower levels open, higher ones locked), and its pause menu is always Resume, Restart, Levels (`#pResume`, `#pRestart`, `#pLevels`, plus `window.gameFeedLevels()` for the feed).
3. Test it (see LESSONS.md, including fast-forward cheat tests; add it to `tools/test_controls.py` and run that; run `python3 tools/speed.py` and keep to LESSONS rule 12: it must be shown to run smoothly before he plays it, because his phone cannot be seen from here), then publish it as an artifact so he can play it; republish the same artifact for each revision.
4. When he approves it: add or update its entry in `games.json` (next number; never renumber; bump `version` on changes), run `python3 tools/ledger.py`, add its build notes and any new lessons to `LESSONS.md`, then commit and push. GitHub runs `tools/check.py`, which loads every game and fails on errors or registry mistakes.
5. Statuses: `in-progress` (being built), `testing` (he's play-testing), `approved` (done), `retired` (removed from the feed, number kept).
6. **A game goes on the app only when Nabeel says so** (his words, 15 Nov 2026: "obviously, you're not gonna put the game on the app until I specifically tell you to... I want to finish designing [it], and I want to be able to say, hey, put this game on the app now"). Building a game, play-testing it, even approving it, never puts it on the app: until he says the word it is a hosted page for him to play and nothing more, and its entry in `games.json` has no `"in_app"`. While it is in `testing`, get everything else ready so that his word is the only thing missing: fill in its `feed` fields (`hook`: one short line for its card; `genre`; `intensity` 1-5; `thumbs` 1 or 2; `start`: the button(s) that begin play, default `["#playBtn"]`; `font`: its title font; `mini`: true once it has its instant start; `live`: a JavaScript expression that is true once he is in live play), and keep `python3 tools/test_controls.py <id>` passing for it. **When he says to put it on the app** (or when a game already on the app changes its look): set `"in_app": true`, run `python3 tools/previews.py <id>` to make its card picture and clip (the clip must be the game's most exciting moment and keep the clip rules in LESSONS, "Every clip is the most exciting moment": write `games/<id>/preview.json` for it and look at the sheet in `out/previews/`), `python3 tools/fonts.py` if it uses a font not yet in `feed/fonts/`, then `python3 tools/test_feed.py` and `python3 tools/test_sync.py` (the second fetches the new game with the phone app's real updater and plays it in the feed from the phone's own files), republish the **Game Feed** page (see below), run `python3 tools/ledger.py`, commit and push. A new game is **not** a core game: leave `"core"` out (only the first six have it; they are the ones inside the phone app, always on the phone). The push is all his phone needs, with nothing for him to install: GitHub takes about six minutes to check and publish; after that the app learns of the game the next time he comes back to it (or taps "Check for new games" in the Games list) and shows it as the next card, marked New, without reloading anything. The game itself is played over the internet: it is fetched when its card comes up, and he can keep it on the phone with **Save** in the Games list. Tell him exactly that, and never tell him to reinstall for a new game. To take a game off the app again, set `"in_app": false`.

## The feed app (built Nov 2026; read "The feed app" in LESSONS.md before changing it)
- **What it is:** `feed/index.html` is the app. One game card at a time, shuffled; swipe up for the next, down for the last; each card plays a short silent clip of the game and a tap starts it; the bar under the card has **Games** (the list, with search, and **Save** on every game that is not a core game: it keeps that game on the phone so it plays with no internet), **Sound** (one switch that silences or restores every game), the swipe handle, **Pause** (while a game is running) and **Pin**. **Core games** (the first six, `"core": true`) are inside the app and always on the phone; every other game is played over the internet unless he has saved it. A paused game shows the same three controls every game has in its own pause menu: **Resume, Restart, Levels**. After about ten games a soft "That's a set" card (PLAYBOOK P27).
- **Where the games come from:** `python3 tools/build_feed.py` builds the whole app into `out/app/` from `games.json`: the feed page, `games.js` (the list), every game marked `"in_app": true` (see step 6 above: only on his word) with the plug (`feed/plug.js`) put at the top of its page, the card pictures and clips, and the fonts. Nothing inside a game has to change for the feed. The same folder is the hosted page and what GitHub publishes for phones; the inside of the Android app is the same build with only the core games' own files (`--core`).
- **The plug** lets the feed start any game (it presses the game's own Play button, `feed.start`), freeze it completely when he swipes away or the app is hidden (frames, timers, clock and sound all stop) and carry on from the same instant. **A tap on a card drops him straight into the action:** the plug calls the game's `window.gameFeedStart('mini')` (every game has one, or needs none; see LESSONS rule 7), so there is no menu, countdown or intro between the tap and play. The feed's Levels button calls the game's `window.gameFeedLevels()`. A game can do more if it wants: `gameFeedPause()`, `gameFeedResume()`, `gameFeedSound(on)`, and `GameFeed.finish({win, level, score})` to report a finished level.
- **Its page:** `feed_app.artifact` in `games.json`. Republish that same artifact (all files in `out/app/`, mode replace) after any change to the feed or to a game in it.
- **Android app:** `android/` is a small wrapper around `out/app/`. On every push to `main`, after the checks pass, GitHub builds `GameFeed.apk` and attaches it to a new Release; the newest is always at `feed_app.apk`. It is signed with `android/signing/release.p12`, whose password is the `KEYSTORE_PASSWORD` Actions secret (only Nabeel can add it; without it GitHub still proves the app builds, but makes no `GameFeed.apk`).
- **New games reach his phone by themselves; anything app-wide comes as a new download.** Both halves are his rules (LESSONS, "New games arrive by themselves"). He will never stop adding games and must not have to update anything to get them: after the checks pass, the `publish` job puts the built app on the **`app-v2`** branch (`feed_app.published`); every time the phone app opens or he comes back to it (and every 10 minutes while it stays open) it reads `manifest.txt` from there and fetches what it keeps into a folder on the phone (`android/.../Sync.java`): the list, the small list pictures, fonts, the core games and the games he has saved. The feed adds new games to what it is showing, as the next cards, marked New, without reloading anything. Any other game's own files are fetched when its card comes up and cleared when the app next starts (LESSONS, "Core games live on the phone"). **The feed page (`feed/index.html`) and the Java in `android/` only reach him inside a new APK**: when either changes, check the new `GameFeed.apk` from outside once GitHub has finished (`python3 tools/check_release.py <build number>`: what is inside it, its version and signing key, what was published, and the real updater against the real address) and tell him plainly that this one needs installing, with the link (`feed_app.apk`). The plug (`feed/plug.js`) travels with the games, so it must always work with an older feed page: only add to what the two say to each other. Never push to or delete the old **`app`** branch: it is frozen at build 18 for the first installed apps, which restart the feed whenever it changes. Read "New games arrive by themselves" and "What went wrong in builds 15 to 18" in LESSONS.md before touching `Sync.java`, `MainActivity.java`, `manifest.txt`, `GameNative` or the publish job.
- **Tests:** `python3 tools/test_feed.py` plays the feed in a phone-sized browser (every game starts from one tap, freezes solid, Pause on the bar, Resume, Restart and Levels on the Paused card, swiping, order, pin, search, break card, back button, phone-app loading). `python3 tools/test_controls.py` plays every game's own basic controls (the level he was on comes back, pause, resume, restart, levels, a win moves the saved level on). `python3 tools/test_sync.py` runs the phone app's real updater against a stand-in for GitHub (first copy, new game, dropped connection, bad files; core games from the app with no internet, a game fetched on demand, saved, let go, cleared at the next start) and then plays the whole journey in the feed with the real updater doing the app's part. `python3 tools/check_android_java.py` compile-checks the wrapper's Java. `python3 tools/check_release.py` checks the newest released `GameFeed.apk` from outside (run it in a session after a push that changes the app; it needs the internet, so GitHub does not run it). GitHub runs the first three after `tools/check.py`, and nothing is published or built unless they pass.
- **What comes next is for later, when he asks:** the "what comes next" brain (now a shuffled deck in `nextGame()`; play history is already kept in `gf.stats` and `gf.log`), taking over a clip at the exact moment he taps it (offered, he chose the instant start instead), and, when there are too many games to keep them all on every phone, fetching a game only when its card comes up.
