# 16. Short Order

## The idea (his words, then mine)
His list (17 Nov 2026): "Cooking." Medium (3), one thumb. A small diner kitchen in 3D, seen from where the cook stands: a steel
counter, a black glass stove with three cast-iron pans, a wooden board and knife, a white diner plate, trays of raw food in front,
the pass with its heat lamps, and a window onto the street with the level's weather and light (rain on the glass, a storm, night).
Order tickets come in along the top; cook each dish right and get it out before the customer walks out.

## How it plays (one thumb, made for cooking)
- Drag food from the tray to where it goes: meat, eggs and pancakes onto a pan; tomato, lettuce and onion onto the board; bun and
  toast straight onto the plate.
- A ring around each pan fills as the side underneath cooks: grey (raw), gold (the golden window), red, black (burnt). Tap the pan to
  flip it; take it off when the second side turns golden too. Eggs are not flipped.
- Sweep back and forth across the board to chop (each sweep is one chop; the board shows how many are left).
- Drag cooked food from the pan, or chopped food from the board, onto the plate; drag the plate up to the pass to serve it.
  Anything can be dragged to the bin.
- The oldest ticket is on the left. Its bar runs from green to red; when it empties, the customer walks out.
- Three walkouts or plates sent back (wrong, raw or burnt) and the shift is over.

## Dishes
Pancakes, Egg on toast (from 1); Burger: bun, patty, lettuce, tomato (from 2); Salmon salad (4); Steak and onions (6);
Pancake stack (8). Every tenth level is the big rush: three more orders.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever (LESSONS rule 0)
Level 1: three orders, one at a time, slow heat, a wide golden window, a minute and more of patience. Then more orders per shift
(up to 14, plus 3 on the big rush), tickets coming closer together, up to four open at once (from 15), pans cooking faster, the
golden window narrower, customers less patient. Help after three losses on a level gives everyone more time.
Robots: an ordinary player wins all of 1 to 20 and 8 in 10 of level 40; a sloppy one all of 1 to 10; a sharp one all of 40
and 2 in 10 of level 80.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves (BLIND in part2): flip a pan, chop, take a random food, plate from a pan, plate from the board, serve, bin the plate.
- A wrong or raw or burnt plate is sent back and costs a life, like a walkout, so serving blindly loses fast; food left alone burns.
- Every blind string of up to four moves on 8 beats on levels 1, 2, 3 and 6: 0 of 77,504 won. Random moves: 0 of 480.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1: put a pancake on, flip it when golden, take it off, serve it. 2: chop a tomato; then a burger, start to finish. 3: two orders
at once (cook one while chopping for the next). Lesson tickets wait three times as long, and nothing is lost during a lesson.

## Engagement factors chosen
P6 every input answers back (sizzle, chop, the knife, the plate clink, the order bell), P13 best tips per level, P18 a streak of
perfect plates, P21 tidy satisfaction (food snaps into its place, the plate fills up as the ticket asks), P30 the order bell and
"PERFECT!" on a golden plate. Sounds from the kit: sizzle bed that rises with the pans, flip, chop, plate clink, the bell.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
The big rush: three pans sizzling, a steak flipped golden, the plate slid up to the pass, the bell and "PERFECT!".
