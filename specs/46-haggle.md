# 46. Haggle

## The idea (his, 18 Nov 2026, set 8)
"A negotiation game where you try to get the lowest price for something. Offer too much and you overpay; offer too little
and you could lose the deal. You do a bunch of rounds and you slowly start to understand the value of things like pawn shop."
2D on purpose: faces, things and prices.

## How it plays (one thumb)
You run a pawn shop. Each customer brings a thing (12 kinds: watches, guitars, rings, cameras, comics, trainers, vases,
coins, consoles, paintings, violins, handbags; three makers each, cheap to fine; mint, good, worn or broken) and names a
price. Their face shows their mood, their cups of tea their patience. The tag says what it is, its state, who they are
("needs the money today", "knows what it is worth", "in a hurry, no questions") and any claim ("It's signed!"). The 🔍
loupe checks claims and marks, the 🔦 UV lamp spots fakes; each look costs a cup. Drag the brass dial to your offer and tap
OFFER: well below what they will take insults them (two cups; a lower offer than your last insults too); a first fair
offer is usually countered; they come down step by step; pay their price and it is yours. ✋ Pass sends them away.
At closing everything sells for its real worth: profit against the target, each item shown paid → sold.

## Levels
Seven customers rising to nine. Claims from level 2, fakes from 3 (more from shady sellers), a "hot today" kind from 4
(worth a third more), new kinds of thing over the levels, better streets (Market Street to Bond Street) with dearer things.
The day is dealt in the morning (fine, middling and cheap things, states and sellers in fixed shares) and the target is a
share of what today's things are really worth, so the mix does not decide the day; skill does. Help: a lower target.

## Systems
The Price Book (every kind of thing you have sold, with the range it fetched: you learn the values, which is the point); the
Back Room (Price guide shows the book while you haggle; Kettle on: one more cup for everyone; Shop window: things sell for
more); ranks from Junk dealer to Tycoon.

## Spamming cannot win
- Blind moves: `o` (offer wherever the dial is, nothing to twice the asking price), `p` (pass), `i` (shine a lamp).
- Random offers overpay, insult, or go down and insult; nobody asks less than a thing is worth: blind 0 of 768 and 480.
  Ordinary robots (who know values roughly, check fine things, open by the seller's type and come up step by step) win
  80-95%.

## Lessons
1. BUY LOW: two good buys in a row. 2. HAGGLE: two under 70% of the worth. 3. SPOT THE FAKE: three right calls with the UV
lamp. Skip tutorial on every lesson; Tutorial on home and pause.

## Engagement factors chosen
P7 risk and reward (one more step down, or they walk); P3 honest feedback (paid → sold at closing for every item); P22 the
Price Book filling; P14 the near goal (customer k of n, the target); P10 sound (the shop bell, the dial's ratchet, the till).

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1: a thing, a price, a dial: everyone knows haggling.
- L2: the bell, the dial ratchet, the cha-ching, the huff.
- L3: at closing every thing shows what you paid and what it was worth.
- L4: customer k of n, the target.
- L5: your best day's profit on each level.
- L6: twelve kinds of thing, claims, fakes, hot items, sellers with different natures.
- L7: check it (and spend patience) or not; push lower or take it.
- L8: Try again is one tap.
- L9: the Price Book, the Back Room.
- L10: your best day on the home card.
