# 59. Origami

## The idea (mine, set 10)
Folding paper animals: the set's calm craft game (intensity 1). Nothing in the feed is like it. 2D on purpose: paper folding
is flat and reads best from straight above, like your own hands on a table.

## How it plays (one thumb)
A square of paper on a wooden table. Put your thumb on the paper and drag: the paper folds along the line halfway between
where your thumb went down and where it is now, so the corner you took follows your thumb, exactly like real paper. A
dashed outline shows the shape to make. Let go when the paper fits it:
- close: the fold is made, snapped neat, and graded PERFECT, NEAT or ROUGH (a crisp crease, a little bell for a perfect);
- far off, or the wrong flap: the paper springs back with a crumple. Three crumples (four on the first levels) and it tears.
The last fold made, the animal comes to life: eyes that blink, a nose, whiskers, a whale's spout, a fish's bubbles; it hops
and goes on your shelf. Six animals: fox, puppy, cat, whale, fish, bird.

## Levels
Early on the corner to take glows and a dot shows where it goes; from level 5 no dot (only the outline), from level 10 no glow
either. The room for error shrinks steadily. From level 13 two models in a row. Four tables (The Tea House, The Garden, The
Temple, The Studio). Help after losses: more room for error, the hints back, one more crumple.

## Systems
The live fold, grading (perfect, neat, rough), crumples and tearing, fading hints, six models, the shelf (every animal you
have made, with your neatest), stars (neatness, no crumples), the Paper Shop (Bone folder, Strong paper, A teacher), ranks
from Beginner to Sensei, best neatness per level.

## Controls
Drag one thumb. That is all.

## Spamming cannot win
- Blind moves: `d` (a drag from anywhere to anywhere on the table), `s` (a short drag), `t` (a tap, which does nothing).
- A random drag is almost never close to the right crease: three crumples and it tears. Blind 0 of 5,696, random 0 of 480.
- In lessons a drag counts only if it starts on the glowing corner (and lesson 1 is a whole fox), so luck cannot pass them.

## Lessons
1. YOUR FIRST FOX: glowing corner onto the dot, three times. 2. MATCH THE OUTLINE: a whole puppy, fitting the outline.
3. NEAT AND PERFECT: two perfect folds.

## Engagement factors chosen
P5 calm flow (no clock, no enemies); P3 a moment that feels good (the paper following the thumb, the crisp crease); P9
collection (the shelf); P2 a run that builds (perfects in a row climb a note); P10 sound (soft paper, the crease, the koto run
when the animal comes to life).

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1: drag a corner: the paper does exactly what paper does.
- L2: it follows your thumb every instant; the crease is crisp.
- L3: the outline shows the target; the grade says how close you were.
- L4: fold N of M, the paper left before it tears, always in sight.
- L5: neatness per level, stars, best.
- L6: six animals, fading hints, two models in a row.
- L7: (calm) how carefully to go: a quick rough fold or a slow perfect one.
- L8: Try again is one tap.
- L9: the shelf of animals you made; the Paper Shop; your rank.
- L10: your neatest on the home card, and the shelf.

## The card clip
A flap lifting after the thumb, PERFECT!, the last fold snapping into a fox, which blinks and hops: FOX!
