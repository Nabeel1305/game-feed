# 62. Paint Drop

## The idea (set 11: his brief)
"The game where you take colours and mash them together": the drop-and-merge game (Suika). Paint blobs drop into a jar; two
of the same colour squash into the next colour, a size bigger. 2D on purpose: a jar seen side on, like the original.

## How it plays (one thumb)
Touch and slide to move the blob in hand along the top (a dotted line shows where it will land, and a ring marks a blob of
its own colour it will land on); let go to drop it. Blobs fall, roll and stack.
- Two blobs of the same colour touching squash into one of the next colour: red, orange, yellow, lime, green, teal, sky,
  blue, violet, pink, rainbow (two rainbows: a masterpiece). A squash can set off another (a chain: the note climbs).
- WET PAINT SQUASHES, DRY PAINT DOES NOT. A blob glistens wet for a moment after it is dropped or made; if it touches its
  own colour while wet they squash. Once dry (matte) it waits: drop a wet blob of its colour onto it.
- The PALETTE: six drops. A drop that leads to no squash dries one; any squash refills them all. All six dry: the paint is
  finished. (Squash, or set up for later? A real decision every drop.)
- The LINE: a blob resting above the dashed line for too long spills the jar.
- Each level asks for a colour (sky to start, blue from level 10). Make it before the paint runs out.
- Grey STONES (from level 6) never squash; a squash right beside one cracks it away.

## Levels
The goal colour (sky, then blue), the jar narrows a little and its line drops, more stones, bigger blobs in the queue from
level 10. Four rooms (The Studio, The Gallery, The Workshop, The Attic). Help after losses: a wider jar and one more palette
drop.

## Systems
Aiming, wet and dry paint, squashes and chains, the palette, the line, stones, the colour chart (every colour on the way to
the goal lights up), paint left, stars (paint to spare), the Art Shop (More paint, Wider jar, Shake), ranks from Dabbler to
Old master, best score per level.

## Controls
Slide and let go. (Shake is a button, once a level, if bought.)

## Spamming cannot win
- Blind moves: `d` (drop where the hand is), `m` (slide somewhere), `l`, `r` (nudge).
- Plain drop-and-merge cannot be made spam-proof: gravity rolls same colours together by itself, and random dropping reaches
  the middle colours easily (measured: blind won most early levels). Three rules, each measured, fix it without changing
  how a careful player plays: wet paint only squashes for a moment (so squashes happen where you aim), the palette (a
  careful player never goes more than five drops without a squash; a random thumb usually goes seven to nine), and the
  queue rarely gives the same colour twice in a row (so dropping in one spot over and over does not stack matches).
- Final tuning, each step measured by the full search: paint stays wet 1.2 s; a drop needs 0.85 s after the last (a
  person aiming drops about once a second; frantic tapping put several wet blobs in the air at once); the early jar is 10.5
  wide with the line 1.3 below its width. Blind: 8 of 13,056 rhythms, 0 of 480 random (it was 169 of 13,056 before the
  queue rule). Careful players win every one of levels 1 to 5.

## Lessons
1. SAME COLOURS SQUASH: drop a red right onto the red, four in a row (one drop at a time, each judged, so luck cannot pass
it). 2. CHAINS: make a yellow in one drop, three times. 3. MIND THE LINE: twelve drops in a full jar without spilling.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK numbers)
P9 squash and stretch (blobs squash on landing, swell when made); P18 combos (chains climb the note and the word); P14 the
goal-gradient (the colour chart lights up colour by colour to the goal); P3 honest near-miss ("you made teal: so close");
P30 the signature: blobs with faces that brighten when they squash, glossy when wet.

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1: drop, same colours squash: seen in two drops.
- L2: the blob follows the thumb; landing squashes; squashes splash and sing.
- L3: the landing line and ring show exactly where it goes; wet and dry show at a glance.
- L4: the colour chart, the palette, paint left.
- L5: the chart shows how far you got; best score per level.
- L6: stones, narrower jars, bigger blobs in the queue, sky then blue goals.
- L7: squash now or set up a chain; where to put the big ones.
- L8: Try again is one tap.
- L9: the Art Shop, your rank.
- L10: best score per level.

## The card clip
A red dropped onto a red, orange onto orange, a chain to green, faces beaming, the colour chart lighting up.
