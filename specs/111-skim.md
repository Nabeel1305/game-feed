# 111. Skimming Stones (my idea, set 20)

## The idea
Mine, to balance set 20 (Joust and Splash Course are loud, Surf is quick): a quiet evening by a lake. Throw a flat stone and
keep it skipping by tapping each time it kisses the water. Intensity 1, one thumb, held upright. Each lake asks for one thing
(so many skips, past a buoy, through floating rings, onto the old jetty), with five stones to do it.

**3D:** yes. The lake at golden hour, pines on the far bank, reeds and a little flag leaning in the wind, and the rings of
ripples each kiss leaves are the whole mood. The light changes with the place: golden Mill Pond, a sunset over Willow Lake,
a misty loch, a lilac dusk on Moon Water.

## Research
I cannot play other games myself, so I read reviews, store pages and the physics:
- **Skipping Stone** (Gamevil; reviews on Pocket Gamer, AppSpy, 148Apps, Gamezebo): a one-button game where you tap as the
  stone meets a shadowed ring on the water; early or late taps cost power, and the stone sinks when it runs out. Reviewers
  loved the instant start and the purity, but called it shallow with only two modes (distance and number of skips). So here:
  the same tap on a ring as the core, but every lake has its own goal, and rings, a buoy, the jetty, wind, lily pads and
  ducks change how you throw.
- **Skipping Stones** (Complex Games; iPhone in Canada review): a tilt-and-flick throw that was easy to understand but hard to
  get consistent; even the same motion gave different skips. So here the throw is a plain drag back with dots showing exactly
  where the stone will land, and nothing random in the throw.
- **SkipIt**, **Drifting Stone**, **Stone Skipper Dash** (store pages): tap "as it kisses the water" to carry speed; easy to
  learn, hard to master; stones to collect. Kept: the tap, the stone collection (unlocked by stars) and upgrades.
- **The physics** (Clanet, Hersen and Bocquet, Nature 2004; Physics World): a flat, spinning stone at a shallow angle skips
  best, and a stone needs a minimum speed to bounce at all. Kept, simply: the flat stone keeps more speed each kiss, a round
  pebble less, and a stone too slow sinks; the heavy stone is pushed less by the wind.

## How it plays (one thumb, upright)
- **Throw:** put your thumb down anywhere and drag back down the screen: the further, the harder (shown in %); a little left
  or right aims the other way, like a catapult. Dots show the first three hops (with perfect taps, before the wind). Let go to
  throw. A drag too short throws nothing. Three stones sit at the bottom: flat slate (skips best), round pebble, heavy granite
  (shrugs off the wind); tap one to pick it.
- **Skip:** as the stone comes down, a ring closes on the spot where it will touch, gold, then green. Tap when it is green:
  PERFECT keeps nearly all its speed (with a glassy chime), GOOD a little less, early, late or no tap loses a lot (the word
  says which). A tap just after the kiss still counts as good. One tap per kiss: extra taps make it wobble. Too slow, it sinks.
- **Lakes:** skips (9 at first), a buoy to pass (48 m at first), floating rings to skip through (one, then up to four), the
  old jetty (a soft throw that slides onto its boards). From level 5 wind, from 6 lily pads, from 8 ducks paddling side to
  side; a lily pad or a duck stops the stone.
- **Win:** the goal met (it wins when that stone comes to rest). Stars by stones to spare: three with three or more.

## Engagement factors
P1 just hard enough (the tap moment narrows with the levels; people who look win the first lakes nearly every time, about
four in five at level 20 and three in five at level 40), P3 honest near-miss (best skips or distance against the goal, and
the word for each kiss: early, late or no tap, from the real timing), P5 teach by doing (one lesson on each of the first three
lakes, a word over every kiss), P16 natural stopping points (one lake a level, an end card with the best), P20 a gentle palette
(golden hour, sunset, mist, dusk).
- L1 understanding: drag back, let go, tap on the green ring. L2 controls: one drag, one tap anywhere, a big moving target.
- L3 fair: the ring shows the moment; the word says early or late. L4 near goal: the bar and the stones left, top.
- L5 improvement: best skips and distance on screen, stars per lake. L6 variety: four kinds of lake, wind, pads, ducks.
- L7 choices: which stone, how hard, where to aim. L8 retry: the next stone is in your hand a second later.
- L9 yours: six stones to unlock (sandstone to lucky gold), upgrades (keen eye, strong arm, deep pocket). L10 your best stars.

## Sound (S1-S11)
Water lapping, birdsong now and then (its own switch), reeds in the wind; a soft whoosh for the throw; each kiss a "plip" a
note higher than the last (a chime above it when perfect), a duller plop for a miss; a gloop when it sinks; a bell for each
ring, a wooden clack for the jetty, a quack for a duck. Music: a slow plucked tune over low notes.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
Blind moves: `t` tap, `h` a hard throw anywhere, `s` a soft throw anywhere, `k` pick a random stone. Only the first tap of a
hop counts and extra taps wobble; a stone thrown blind with no good taps goes about 20-40 m in 3-4 skips; the first goals
(9 skips, 48 m) need real taps. The first kiss comes later for a harder throw, so a fixed rhythm cannot land on it.
Measured: every blind string of up to four moves (13,056 games) won 4 (0.03%), the closest string 6 of 100; random moves 0 of
480; 90 s of blind tapping does not finish the first lesson (three good taps in a row).
