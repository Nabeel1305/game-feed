# Police Chase (game 14), second version: his review of 23 Nov 2026

## His words (critical flaw, from the Game Review page and the chat)
"You're supposed to be chasing a criminal but the game doesn't make it clear who the criminal is. There should be a clear sign that
this is the car. This is the only car you should be chasing. And once the criminal gets out to run the running graphics are just
awful. Plus the skyscrapers randomly disappear if you play the game for long enough. Plus the car controls should be a joystick
with a button for brake, acceleration and reverse, and a city shouldn't be just a straight line but turns and corners."

## What each point becomes
1. **Who to chase**: a tall red arrow over his car, a red ring on the road under it, a bright orange car (traffic is dull), and
   when he is off the screen an arrow at the edge with the distance. The same red arrow is over him on foot.
2. **On foot**: seen from behind your own officer (it was first person: a black lower half of the screen and a dark blob ahead).
   Both runners are built with knees and elbows, lean into the run, pump their arms, bounce a bag; the alley is lit and has
   colour (doors, shutters, bins, puddles).
3. **Skyscrapers vanishing**: found. The buildings are one instanced mesh re-placed as you drive; three.js keeps the bounding
   sphere it worked out at the start, so once you are far from where you began the whole mesh is culled. `frustumCulled = false`
   on every re-placed instanced mesh. (Checked in every other game: see the audit note of 23 Nov.)
4. **Controls**: left thumb, a floating stick that steers; right thumb, GAS and BRAKE (held at a standstill, BRAKE reverses:
   the convention every driving game uses, and it is written on the button).
5. **A city with corners**: a grid of streets (one every 84 m, 16 m wide), blocks of buildings between. He turns at junctions; you
   must brake for corners (at top speed the car cannot make a right angle), which is where you gain or lose him.

## Research (I cannot play other games; these are studied, not played)
- Driving on phones: the common layout is steering on the left and gas and brake pedals under the right thumb, with brake
  doubling as reverse (Escape Road's keys: "S/Down arrow: Brake/reverse", drifted.com/escape-road-2; CarX's on-screen gas and
  brake, phonearena.com drifting games round-up).
- Players dislike having the driving taken away: gsmarena's Asphalt 9 review says it makes you "a spectator in your own game".
- City chases read best from above and behind in a low-poly grid city (drifted.com on Escape Road: the top-down camera "keeps
  everything clean and readable").
- Marking the suspect: a large marker high above the car, visible from the pursuer (BeamNG forum, "Vehicle tracking with a
  marker": a red arrow pointing down above the vehicle).
- Attention on the street, not a mini-map (allthings.how on GTA 6's GPS): so an edge arrow, no map.

## Rules kept
The PIT (hit his side or back corner; straight into his bumper does nothing), three lives, then on foot (dodge, jump, slide,
tackle) and the cuffs: unchanged. Wall scrapes cost speed, not a life; a hard head-on wall hit or a traffic crash costs a life.

## Longevity L1-L10 (a line each)
L1 the red arrow says who; the stick and two labelled pedals say how. L2 the car turns as the stick moves; braking bites. L3 he
slows for corners as you must. L4 "PIT 1 of 2" and the distance are on screen. L5 best time and stars kept. L6 the grid, his
turns, traffic and weather change every chase. L7 brake late or early; which side to ram. L8 retry at once. L9 your rank and
stars. L10 best time per level shown at the end.
