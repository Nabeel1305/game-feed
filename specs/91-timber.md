# 91. Timber

## The idea (mine, for set 16)
This one is my pick for set 16. The set so far is Sumo (intensity 4), Memory Lane (1), Spy (3) and Mocktail Bar (1), so it can
take a game in the middle. It brings a genre the feed does not have yet (forestry) and a one-thumb control the feed has not
used yet. You are a faller in a forest clearing. You drop each tree exactly into the open strip marked for it, the way real
fellers do: a notch on the side it should fall to, a back cut from the other side, and a strip of wood (the hinge) left
uncut to steer it down. It is tense but not fast. Everything happens slowly, and then all at once when it tips. Intensity 2,
one thumb.
3D: the falling tree is the whole show (it creaks, tips, swings down about its hinge and lands with a thump). The forest,
the weather and the light change by place.

## Research (21 Nov 2026; I cannot play these games myself, so I studied guides and reviews)
- How real felling works (OSHA logging eTool; Husqvarna Chainsaw Academy): a notch (undercut) faces the way the tree should
  fall. The back cut comes from the other side and stops short, leaving a hinge about a tenth of the trunk's width. The hinge
  holds the tree to the stump for most of the fall and steers it. If you cut through the hinge you lose that control. A lean,
  the overhang and the wind pull a tree their way, and felling against them takes more effort. What I took: the whole game is
  built on notch, back cut and hinge. The ring shows the good notch depth (about a third) and the good hinge (green). A lean
  and gusts pull the tree. A thick hinge leaves it standing, so you need a wedge. A cut-through hinge lets the tree twist.
- Timberman (Pocket Gamer, TouchArcade, App Store): one tap per chop, very quick rounds and many unlockable characters, which
  makes for an addictive "one more" loop. What I took: rounds of a minute or so, a retry in a tap, and saw colours you
  unlock. I did not take the frantic tapping: this game rewards stopping at the right moment, not speed.
- Lumberjack's Dynasty (reviews: Movies Games and Tech, TheXboxHub, PC Invasion): players love the calm work and the
  atmosphere. The complaints were felled trees that stayed standing until shoved, and camera trouble. PC Gamer's ranking of
  tree-felling in games praised a tree that falls all the way with a thud that feels weighty. What I took: a tree that falls
  for real, with a heavy landing (thump, shake, dust, a small bounce). The camera behind the tree always looks along the
  felling line, and it lifts to watch the fall.

## How it plays
- Drag across the picture to turn the felling line (painted on the ground) toward the strip; the camera turns with it.
- HOLD NOTCH (the ring fills from the felling side; let go around a third). The line is then fixed.
- HOLD SAW: the back cut comes from behind; the wood left turns green on the ring when it is the right hinge; let go.
- If it still stands, the left button is now WEDGE: tap to drive wedges until it tips. "TIMBER!"
- It lands: PERFECT, GOOD, IN THE STRIP, OFF THE STRIP or HIT; a miss or a hit costs a chance, says why, and the same tree
  comes back at once. Every tree in its strip wins the level.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever (LESSONS rule 0)
Three trees and two spare chances on levels 1-3. After that, three to five trees and one spare chance. The strip narrows
(3 m to 1.6 m half-width). New things arrive one at a time: side lean (4), oaks (4), wind (5), hikers (6), birches (7),
dead snags with brittle hinges (10) and giant firs (13). The guides fade too: the predicted landing is shown up to level 4,
then only the lean arrow up to 12, then nothing. In the robot tests an ordinary player wins 95-100% of games up to level 13 and about
half at level 40; a sharp player wins about three quarters at 40.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- The blind moves are a random drag (`a`), NOTCH (`n`), SAW (`s`), let go (`u`) and WEDGE (`w`). Every pattern was tried at
  every pace on levels 1, 2, 3 and 6: 8,700 blind games and none won. Holding SAW through cuts the hinge, so the tree goes
  where its lean likes. The line starts pointing away from the strip.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1. NOTCH, THEN SAW (the line already points at the strip). 2. AIM THE FALL (drag it into the strip, away from a fence).
3. THE WEDGE (a tree leaning back sits on its hinge). A lesson tree that misses simply comes back; nothing is lost.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK)
- P4 Fair, readable failure: the ring shows notch and hinge as you cut, and each miss says why ("the hinge was cut through",
  "the lean pulled it", "a gust pushed it", "the hiker was under it").
- P6 Every input answers back: the saw's buzz drops as it bites, chips fly, the hinge creaks, wedges ring, and the phone
  pulses at the crack and the landing.
- P3 Honest near-miss: the landing grade comes from how far off the middle line the tree really lies.
- P15 Nested goals: this tree, this level's stars (no misses, half of them perfect), saw colours, the shop and your rank.

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1 Drop it in the strip. L2 Drag, hold, let go, tap. L3 You can see the lean, the wind and the path before you cut.
- L4 The strip and its stakes are right ahead. L5 Stars and your best coins per level. L6 Lean, wind, hikers, kinds of tree
  and things to miss change what you do. L7 How far to aim against the lean, and whether to wedge now or wait for a lull.
- L8 The same tree comes back at once. L9 Saw colours, shop upgrades and rank. L10 Your best coins per level.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
The hinge creaks, "TIMBER!", and a tall pine swings down into the strip and lands with a thump and a burst of dust.
