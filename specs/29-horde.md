# 29. Horde

## The idea (his words, then mine)
Set 5 is all mine (his list is built; his words for it: "Ok next 5 make it good please"). The most addictive shape in phone
games of the last few years: a survivor. One thumb moves; the hero fights by himself; every level-up offers three powers and
each run builds a different hero. Very intense (5), one thumb, 3D: a crowd of monsters closing round you only works when you
can see the whole clearing round you, at night, under moonlight and storm.

## How it plays (one thumb)
- **Drag anywhere to move** (a floating stick). The hero attacks by himself with every weapon he carries.
- Monsters drop **blue crystals**; they spill a little away from where the monster fell and fade after a while: go and get
  them. Fill the bar to **level up**: the night stops and you **pick one of three cards** (tap).
- **Six weapons, five ranks each:** sword (the swing in front), magic bolts (the nearest monster; the boss first), orbiting
  blades, a holy aura (burns those close; at rank 5 it slows them), lightning from the sky, axes thrown over the crowd.
- **Six blessings:** might, haste, swiftness, a big heart, a magnet, armour. Four weapons and four blessings at most.
- **The beacons:** three braziers round the clearing; stand by one until it burns (a ring fills). The light draws monsters.
  When all three burn, **the boss comes**: the Bone King, the Swamp Hag or the Iron Golem by turn. Its red ring is a slam: get
  out before it lands; it also fires fans of bolts. Kill it and the night is won.
- **Monsters:** bats (fast, weak, in threes), zombies, skeletons, archers (they stop and draw: a red line shows where the arrow
  will fly), ghosts (they slip through the crowd), brutes (huge), and golden elites.
- **Standing still, the horde gathers round you:** keep moving.

## The systems that carry over (set 4's rule)
- **Gold and the armoury:** monsters drop gold now and then, the boss more. Six upgrades that stay: might, vitality, swift
  boots, greed (more gold), long reach (crystals from further), and a phoenix feather (rise once with half your health).
- **Rank:** Squire, Watchman, Monster hunter, Slayer, Champion, Legend of the night.
- **Places:** the graveyard, the dead forest, the swamp, the bone desert (five levels each); dusk, night, storm and mist.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever
More and tougher monsters, more kinds (skeletons from 2, archers 3, brutes 4, ghosts 5), elites, a tougher boss, beacons that
take longer to light. Robots: ordinary all of 1 to 5 and most of 10 to 20; sharp most of 40.

## Spamming cannot win
Blind moves: the stick held one of four ways, let go, a card picked without reading. First found: a survivor fights by itself,
so blind running won 12-27% and standing still won too. Now the night is won only by lighting three beacons and killing the
boss (no clock to outlast), crystals spill away from where monsters fall and fade (the hero must go and get them), and
standing still makes the horde gather round you. Blind strings: 0; random moves 0 of 180.

## Lessons
1: move, and let him fight; collect crystals to the first level-up. 2: pick your power, twice. 3: archers' red lines, then a
small boss.

## Engagement factors chosen
P6 every input answers back (the swing, monsters popping, crystals tinkling), P12 celebrations scaled (ELITE DOWN, MASSACRE, the
boss falling with a flash and a long shake), P15 nested goals (the next level-up, the next beacon, the boss, the next rank),
P17 rare surprises (golden elites), P18 combos (a note higher for each monster in a quick run). Beside them, set 4's systems:
the armoury and rank, and the level-up choice itself.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
Lightning and blades tearing through a packed crowd as the third beacon lights.
