# 33. Rodeo

## The idea (his words, then mine)
Set 5, mine: the intense, two-thumb game of the set (4), 3D: a dusty arena at sundown, strings of lights, a big dark bull,
eight seconds that feel like a minute.

## How it plays (two thumbs: hold left, hold right)
- You sit on the bull like a stick balanced on a hand: you tip all the time. **Hold the left half** of the screen to lean left,
  **the right half** to lean right, **both** to grip (a throw moves you less while your grip lasts; it runs out and comes back
  slowly).
- **The bull tells you what is coming:** his head swings out to the side he is about to throw you (a buck); he drops a shoulder
  before a spin (a long pull: grip it, or lean against it); his back end rises before a kick (short and hard); old bulls fake
  it with a shake of the head. **Lean the other way just before the throw lands** and you ride it.
- **Eight seconds** to the horn. **The judges mark the ride** out of 100: half for keeping the dot in the green (the balance
  arc at the bottom), half for every buck met. 65 to count; 78 and 88 for more stars.

## The systems that carry over (set 4's rule)
- **The tack shop:** rosin glove (grip lasts longer), leather chaps (tip further before you fall), bull rope (grip holds
  tighter), silver spurs (more coins and score), a lucky horseshoe (once a ride you hang on). **Rank:** Greenhorn to Rodeo
  legend. Towns every five levels (Dry Creek to the Las Vegas Finals); a champion bull every tenth.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever
Bucks only at first, with an arrow showing which way and which side to hold (levels 1 to 5); spins from 2, kicks from 4, fakes
from 6; bucks come harder and closer together, the tells get shorter, the bull wobbles more and you balance on a sharper point.

## Spamming cannot win
Blind thumbs: hold left, hold right, hold both, let go, on any beat. A buck is only ridden if you were already leaning against
it as it came (half of the moment before it lands), so dithering does not meet it; grip runs out; and a ride that survives by
luck is too wild for the judges. Blind strings 0 of 13,056, random 0 of 480.

## Lessons
1: lean against him (he leans you one way with an arrow: hold the other side until he stops, three in a row). 2: the buck
(three bucks in a row). 3: grip the spin (two in a row).

## Engagement factors chosen
P4 fair, readable failure (the tell, the arrow on early levels, TOO WILD: NO SCORE), P8 shake and permanence (the arena shakes
with every buck, the dust), P12 celebrations scaled (GOOD, NICE, WHAT A RIDE, the horn and the crowd), P13 visible personal
best (the judges' score and stars), P24 run length to fit the moment (eight seconds).

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
A big buck met: the bull's back end high, dust flying, the rider leaning hard against it with his hat on and one hand up.
