# 63. Pour

## The idea (set 11: his brief)
A colour sort, like Water Sort: really simple, and calm (intensity 1). 2D on purpose: glasses on a shelf read best side on.

## How it plays (one thumb)
Tap a glass to pick it up (it lifts and glows), tap another to pour: the colour on top pours (as much of it as fits) onto the
same colour or into an empty glass, the glass gliding over, tipping at its mouth, a stream running in. Sort every colour into
a glass of its own; a full glass of one colour gets a cork. Every colour carries a faint mark of its own (● ▲ ■ ◆ ★ ...), so
it can be played without telling colours apart.
- Moves: more than the puzzle needs (the solver's route times a margin), so there is room to think but not to wander.
- UNDO takes back a pour (two or three a level).
- HIDDEN layers (from level 6): grey with "?", they show their colour when uncovered.
- A pour that cannot happen wobbles back: free once, another soon after costs a move, then two.
- No pour left anywhere: undo if you can, otherwise it ends ("no pours left"), never a game that just sits there.

## Levels
3 colours, then 4, 5, up to 10; hidden layers from level 6 and more of them as levels rise; a smaller margin of moves.
Every puzzle is generated at random and kept only if the solver sorts it (which also gives the best route shown as "best
in N"). Four rooms (Lab, Café, Garden, Night Bar). Help after losses: more moves and an extra undo.

## Systems
Pouring, the solver's route, moves and undo, hidden layers, the miss rule, corks and the rising chime, stars (sorted in the
best route's number of pours, or within a quarter more), the Supply Shelf (More undos, A spare glass, More moves), ranks from
Beginner to Alchemist, fewest pours per level.

## Controls
Tap a glass, tap another. Undo is a button.

## Spamming cannot win
- Blind moves: `t` (tap a glass somewhere), `u` (undo).
- Most blind taps are wrong pours, which cost more and more moves; the few valid pours wander. Blind 0 of 1,984, random 0
  of 480. In lessons, a second quick wrong pour starts the lesson again, and so does running past the pours to spare.

## Lessons
1. POUR: fill two glasses with one colour each. 2. MAKE ROOM: use the empty glass to sort three colours. 3. UNDO: a puzzle
with a trap, five undos.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK numbers)
P21 tidy-up satisfaction (a glass filling, the cork, the chime); P18 the rising chime with every glass done; P14 progress
("3 of 6 sorted", moves left); P13 visible best (fewest pours, against the best route); P20 gentle palettes.

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1: tap, tap, pour: obvious from one go.
- L2: the glass lifts, glides, tips and pours; the level rises.
- L3: every rule shows itself; a wrong pour wobbles and says why.
- L4: sorted count, moves left, the best route.
- L5: fewest pours per level against the best route.
- L6: more colours, hidden layers, less room.
- L7: which glass to free first; when to spend the empty glass.
- L8: Try again is one tap.
- L9: the Supply Shelf, your rank.
- L10: your fewest pours on each level.

## The card clip
A glass tipping and pouring red onto red, the cork popping on, the chime, then the last glass sorted.
