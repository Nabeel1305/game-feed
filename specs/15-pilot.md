# 15. Pilot

## The idea (his words, then mine)
His list (17 Nov 2026): "Pilot: land a plane safely, through turns and weather." Medium (3), two thumbs. An airliner on final
approach to a countryside airport (fields, woods, hills, a town lit at night), seen from just behind; the runway with its real
markings, edge and centre lights, approach lights and the four glide-path lights (PAPI).

## How it plays (two thumbs)
Right thumb: the column, a stick wherever it goes down: left/right banks and turns; pulled back (down the screen) raises the nose,
pushed forward lowers it; let go and the nose holds where it is. Left thumb: the throttle lever. Follow the glide path (two white,
two red), keep on the centre line (point into a crosswind), speed in the green, then flare: pull back just before the wheels touch,
power off. Touch down softly, wings level, not sliding sideways, on the runway, in its first part. The glide itself comes down at
3.8 m/s, harder than any gear takes, so every landing needs a flare.

## Levels
Further out, starting off the centre line (a turn to line up) from flight 3, crosswind from flight 2, turbulence, a narrower
landing area, a softer touchdown needed; weather by flight (fog, rain, storm, night).

## Spamming cannot win
Blind column and throttle moves: 15 of 77,504 (a fast pull-push on a beat sometimes flares by luck: 7 in 100 on flights 1-3, under
the one-in-ten line); random 0 of 480.

## Lessons
1: keep on the centre line (the plane holds height and speed), flare (it flies the glide for you). 2: follow the glide path and
land (it holds the speed). 3: the throttle: land it all yourself.

## Engagement factors chosen
P4 fair failure (every crash says why; warnings say what to do), P6 every input answers back (the plane, the lever, the stick),
P13 softest touchdown per flight, P12 a butter landing celebrated, P30 the radio-altimeter calls and the glide-path lights.
