# 106. Pottery Wheel (mine, set 19)

## The idea
Mine, to balance set 19 (Sandworm 4, Skydive 2, Detective 1, Convoy 3; three of them two-thumb): a calm, one-thumb craft
game with a real feel to it. A customer's order hangs on a card (a bowl, a cup, a vase, a jug, a bottle, a wide dish, a tall
vase, a moon jar, each stretched a little differently, and a glaze colour). Throw it on the wheel: centre the clay, pull
the walls up, shape them to the order's outline, trim the rim, glaze and fire. Intensity 1, one thumb, upright.

**3D:** yes: the spinning clay is the pleasure of it (a real lathe of the profile you make, wet and glossy, then glazed).

## How it plays (one thumb)
- **Centre:** hold on the clay and drag down a little: the needle shows the pressure; keep it in the green until the wobble
  stops (too hard squashes it back).
- **Pull:** drag up the wall from the bottom to the top; each pull makes the pot taller and the wall thinner (the same clay).
  The trail is green when steady, red when too fast: too fast tears it and it slumps.
- **Shape:** tap SHAPE, then touch the pot and move in or out: the wall follows your thumb at that height (a soft bump a few
  centimetres wide), while the wheel turns. Follow the dotted outline of the order.
- **Trim:** tap TRIM and touch a height: the wire cuts the rim there.
- **Done:** the match is shown; choose the glaze they asked for; it fires (a rare kiln surprise: crackle, speckle, drips,
  gold flecks).
- **Walls gauge:** thin walls cannot be tall, nor lean out far; under a margin it warns, then the pot slumps (fresh clay).
  Three slumps and the order is lost.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever (LESSONS rule 0)
Bowls and cups first, then every shape in turn; the match needed rises from 72% to 91%; pulls tear more easily; the walls
must be a little thicker for the same height. Help after three losses: a lower match is enough.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves: `h` hold with any pressure, `r` let go, `u` a pull of any speed and length, `s` shape anywhere to any
  radius, `t` trim anywhere, `p` back to pulling, `d` done, `g` any glaze.
- Why they lose: the order's outline has to be followed at every height (a plain cylinder scores well under the goal), and
  random fast pulls tear and slump. Proved: 0 of 1,408 blind strings, 0 of 120 random.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1. **CENTRE THE CLAY** twice. 2. **PULL THE WALLS UP**: three steady pulls (a tear starts the count again). 3. **SHAPE TO THE
ORDER**: shape, done, glaze and fire a pot at 70% or more.

## Research (I cannot play other games; I read about them)
- **Let's Create! Pottery** (TouchArcade, Gamezebo, AppSpy, Screenwise): a lump of clay on a spinning wheel shaped with a
  finger, then fired and decorated; praised as zen, relaxing, natural to control, with objectives and orders (Aunt Chloe's
  requests) giving each pot a purpose; TouchArcade notes it never makes you keep balance or deal with the things that make a
  real pot collapse (https://toucharcade.com/2010/07/19/lets-create-pottery-hd-review-all-the-fun-of-making-pottery-without-the-mess/ ,
  https://www.gamezebo.com/reviews/lets-create-pottery-review/ , https://www.creativebloq.com/app/review-lets-create-pottery-hd-app-iphone-and-ipad-5132700).
  So: the same calm thumb-on-clay feel and orders, plus the real steps it leaves out (centring, pulls that can tear, walls
  that slump), which give it a skill to get better at.
- **Wheel-throwing guides** (Ceramic Arts Network, Colorado State's ceramics manual, LibreTexts): centring comes first and
  matters most; then opening, then pulling the walls in steady lifts, slower as the pot gets taller; walls pulled too thin
  cannot hold themselves up and the pot collapses; trim an uneven rim
  (https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/six-top-tips-for-beginners-learning-how-to-wheel-throw ,
  https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/University_of_the_Pacific/Ceramics/05%3A_The_Potters_Wheel/5.03%3A_Creating_Basic_Forms-_Cylinders_Bowls_Plates).
  So: the same order of steps, each its own gesture; fast pulls tear; thin, tall or leaning walls slump; a trim tool.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK)
P6 every input answers back (the clay follows the thumb as the wheel turns, wet sounds), P13 visible personal best, P17 rare
harmless surprises (the kiln's crackle, speckle, drips, gold flecks), P21 tidy-up satisfaction (a pot coming true to its
outline). Sound rules S1-S11. Longevity L1-L10: touch the clay in the first second; hold, drag, touch; the match and the
walls gauge always visible; the order card on the wall; the match after every pot; eight shapes in turn; when to trim,
how far to push; TRY AGAIN at once; clays by stars, the kit bag; best per level.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
The clay rising under a steady pull, then shaped into a vase, glazed, and coming out of the kiln with a crackle.
