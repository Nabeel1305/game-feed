# 96. Beach Watch

## The idea (mine, set 17)
You are the lifeguard in the tower. Swimmers play in the surf; rip currents, swimming too far and offshore wind put them in
danger; some just tire. Whistle people back before it turns to trouble; when someone is in trouble, go; bring them in the
smart way (across the rip, not up it); plant red flags on the rips. The skill is *reading the water*: real trouble is quiet.

**Why it fits set 17:** Hockey (5) and Tennis (4) are the two intense games; Metro Map (2) and Bin Round (1) are calm. This is
an intensity 3 watching-and-deciding game, one thumb, a genre the feed does not have (rescue / watch).
**3D:** the view from the tower is the game (real lifeguards watch from up high because rips are easier to see from above),
with the sea, the breaking waves and the sky changing by beach.

## Research (I cannot play other games; I read about them, and about real rips)
- Lifeguard games on the App Store are mostly driving-and-swimming simulators with poor ratings
  (e.g. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1206360911 , https://apps.apple.com/app/id768605687 ); the most interesting part in game
  jams is *spotting who is in trouble*: an itch.io player enjoyed "trying to identify who is drowning and who isn't"
  (https://itch.io/post/16357830), another found it hard to learn what to look for and wanted a tutorial
  (https://itch.io/post/13747079), and a third found a two-minute round too long (https://itch.io/post/6430887/view-in-topic).
  So: spotting is the core, three short lessons teach the signs, and a shift is 70 to 115 seconds.
- Real drowning is quiet (the Instinctive Drowning Response: no waving or shouting at first), as the "Spot the Drowning Child"
  simulator shows (https://thesilverlining.com/businessblog/blog/can-you-beat-this-drowning-simulator). So trouble has three
  stages: low in the water with the head back (easy to miss), then struggling, then one arm up.
- Rip currents (National Geographic, NOAA Ocean Today, NWS): a dark gap in the breaking waves that stays put; easier to see from
  higher up; the escape is to swim *parallel* to the shore and then in, not straight back against it.
  https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/rip-currents-explainer , https://oceantoday.noaa.gov/fullmoon-ripcurrent/welcome.html ,
  https://www.weather.gov/wrn/spring-article-surfs-up-stay-aware — so rips are gaps in the foam with foam drifting out, and
  coming in up a rip is slow.

## How it plays (one thumb: a tap)
- **Tap someone drifting into danger** (in a rip, past the orange buoys, an inflatable in offshore wind): a whistle, they come back.
- **Tap someone in trouble**: you run and swim out (you can't be sent again while you're out). Reach them and take hold.
- **Tap the beach** where you will bring them in. Straight back up a rip is slow; across it first is quicker. (No tap within 3.5 s:
  straight in.)
- **Tap the gap in the waves** to plant a red flag (two per shift from beach 2): swimmers keep out of that rip.
- **Tap someone who is fine**: you swim out for nothing and must get back up the tower before you can act again (a false
  alarm). Tapping open water costs a moment. Miss two and the beach closes; get through the shift to win.

## Levels: easy first, then harder for ever (LESSONS rule 0)
Beach 1: 8 people, one rip, four tired swimmers to bring in, 19 s before the jet ski has to step in, 9.5 s to whistle a
drifter. Further on: more people (to 15), two or three rips, offshore wind from beach 3, up to 12 tired swimmers, trouble
lasts 11 s and drifting 5 s. Robots: an ordinary player wins all of beaches 1-3, 0.8-0.9 to beach 20, 0.5 at 40.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
Blind moves: `t` a tap anywhere on beach and sea, `s` a tap on whichever swimmer is nearest the thumb. Tapping people at random
sends you out on false alarms (and back up the tower), so the real trouble is missed. Robots: 1,984 blind rounds and 480
random ones, none won.

## Lessons of the first three beaches
1. BRING THEM IN (twice; a way in up the rip does not count). 2. WHISTLE THEM BACK (three in a row). 3. FLAG THE RIPS (both
gaps). In a lesson a wrong tap starts the count again, so tapping blindly for 90 s finishes none.

## Engagement factors chosen
P4 fair, readable failure (the three stages of trouble; the end card says what to watch for), P5 teach by doing (the lessons
are real rescues), P12 celebrations scaled ("SAFE!", a chord and a little applause; three stars for a clean shift), P17 rare
harmless surprises (offshore wind, gulls). Sound rules S1-S11; a quiet pulse in the music while someone is in trouble.
Longevity L1-L10: L1 the lessons; L2 one tap does everything; L3 trouble shows in stages; L4 the shift bar; L5 saved and
whistled on screen, best coins per beach; L6 more rips, wind, busier beaches; L7 whom first, which way in, where to flag;
L8 Try again at once; L9 your kit colours and the kit bag (fins, binoculars, rescue board); L10 your best per beach.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
The lifeguard swimming out across the rip, a swimmer with one arm up, the foam breaking either side of the dark gap.
