# 88. Memory Lane

## The idea (his words, then mine)
His words (20 Nov 2026, idea 23, full text in `specs/his-list-2026-11-20.md`): "challenge the player's memory by briefly
showing them something, hiding it and asking them to recall or recreate what they saw." With directions for the view,
controls, the round, variety, instructions, progression, details, feedback, scoring, sound, vibration, replay, modes and
the session; built from them point by point (below). 2D: a clean card with big things reads better than a 3D room here
(his own words ask for "large objects and minimal distractions").

## Research (21 Nov 2026; I cannot play these myself, so I studied how they are described)
- Lumosity's Memory Matrix: tiles light up for a moment and flip back; the screen shows how many there were; a perfect trial
  adds one tile, a miss keeps the number (help.lumosity.com). Lumosity says its games adapt so the challenge stays "not too
  easy, not too hard". What I took: say how many to find ("TAP THE 4 YOU SAW · 1 OF 4"), and grow one thing at a time.
- Simon (Baer and Morrison, 1978): a series of lights and tones to repeat; each light has its own tone, and a longer
  sequence is the difficulty; compliments at milestones (Wikipedia, IEEE Spectrum). What I took: each thing in IN ORDER has
  its own note (pentatonic, so any order sounds kind), and the sequence is what grows.
- What I took overall: say what to remember before the look; show the answer beside yours afterwards; never time ordinary play.

## His directions, point by point
- View and environment: a calm dark background, one big scene card in six themes (Mia's desk, Grandma's tea tray, windows
  on Cherry Street, the toy shop shelf, a picnic, a rock pool), big things on plates.
- Controls: tap to choose (WHICH WERE THERE?), drag back to place or tap the thing then its place (PUT THEM BACK), tap in
  the order they lit up (IN ORDER), one tap for WHAT'S MISSING? and WHAT CHANGED?. One way at a time: levels 1 to 5
  introduce one each (lessons on 1 to 3, a tip on 4 and 5).
- Basic round: what to remember (the things, their places, the order, everything), the look (a bar runs down; GOT IT ends it
  early), a curtain falls with a soft cue, then the question. Level 1: three things.
- Challenge variety: positions, sequence, missing item, what changed (a look-alike in its place, or two swapped), plus which
  were there.
- Clear instructions: the brief card names exactly what to remember and the question that follows.
- Progression: one factor at a time, in turn every three levels: more things (3 to 10), look-alikes (same family, other plate
  colours), a shorter look. Every tenth level has all five kinds.
- Interesting details: each scene has a little story under it ("Grandma's tea tray: tea, biscuit and pear, and more").
- Fair feedback: after every answer, WHAT WAS THERE beside YOUR ANSWER, rings green and red, and one line on what went wrong.
- Scoring: accuracy first (80% over five rounds passes, all right for three stars); Timed mode (home screen) adds a bonus for
  quick answers; ordinary play is never timed.
- Sound and music: gentle music-box music, quieter while you look; a soft falling cue when the look ends; climbing plucks for
  right answers; a note for each thing in a sequence.
- Phone vibration: a light pulse for each completed answer and the curtain; Settings turn it off or soften it.
- Replay appeal: fresh scenes every time; personal bests kept separately for each kind (the most things all right); mixed
  rounds from level 6.
- Modes: Look: normal / longer / longest on the home screen (relaxed), and the levels themselves as the challenge.
- Session structure: five rounds a level, the result card with each round's score, NEXT LEVEL or PLAY AGAIN at once.

## His rules for every game (20 Nov 2026), kept
- Big targets below the card, nothing over it; fun in ten seconds (level 1 is three things); autosave, pause; separate Music,
  Sound effects and Vibration; original scenes.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves: `a` a random answer, `f` always the first answer, `c` go on. 5,696 blind games, 3 won; random, 480, none.
- A level needs 80% over five rounds; a random choice among twice as many things, a random order, or a random tap on a scene
  scores far lower. Lessons need two right in a row.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1. WHICH WERE THERE? 2. PUT THEM BACK. 3. IN ORDER.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK)
- P3 Honest near-miss: your answer beside the real scene, every difference ringed.
- P5 Teach by doing: one way of answering per level for the first five.
- P13 Visible personal best: best points per level, best per kind of round.
- P12 Celebrations scaled: SPOT ON! for one round, sparkles for a perfect one, PERFECT MEMORY! and three stars for a level.

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1 Look, then answer. L2 Tap and drag. L3 Always what to remember first. L4 Five dots, NEED 80%. L5 Personal bests per kind.
- L6 Five kinds, six themes, look-alikes. L7 GOT IT early or look longer. L8 PLAY AGAIN in one tap. L9 Stars per level.
- L10 Your best kept per kind.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
The curtain falling on a busy desk, then a perfect answer: every ring green, PERFECT! and the sparkles.
