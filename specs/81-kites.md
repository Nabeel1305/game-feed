# 81. Kite Fighter

## The idea (mine, for set 14)
Set 14 had Sprint (5, two thumbs), Bus Driver (3, two thumbs), Cake Designer and Paint It (1, one thumb each). The fifth needed
to be a different kind of game at intensity 3 with one thumb. Kite fighting (Basant in Lahore, Uttarayan in India, pipa
combate in Brazil) is a festival sport played on rooftops across the world, and it has a control that is both simple and
deep: you only ever pull the line or give it slack.

## Research (20 Nov 2026; I cannot play these myself, so I studied store pages, reviews and guides)
- Pipa Combate 3D, CS Diamantes Pipas, Basant The Kite Fight, Patang Baaz, Kite Fighting: cut rival kites' lines in the sky;
  "Khech" (pull) and "Dhil" (give slack) are the two moves; giving slack makes the kite turn, pulling makes it go forward the
  way it faces; who wins depends on angle, line and technique; double, triple cuts are bonuses; sharp manjha cuts faster and
  thick line is harder to cut; cut kites fall and can be caught. (App Store, Steam and Playgama pages)
- Reviews of Kite Fighting complain the controls were hard to understand, with no instructions. (App Store reviews)
- What I took: one thumb, hold = pull, let go = slack (the real two moves, nothing else); an arrow on your kite showing where a
  pull will take it; a clear rule for a crossing (pull and keep moving; the kite above has the edge); multi-cut bonuses,
  catching falling kites, line and kite upgrades; lessons that teach each move.

## How it plays (one screen, one thumb)
- Hold anywhere: PULL. The kite darts the way its nose points (the arrow), against a little gravity. Let go: SLACK. The kite
  turns on the spot and drifts with the wind. The line takes a quarter of a second to tighten, so a flick is only a tug.
- When your line crosses a rival's (PECH!), both saw: the one pulling while its kite moves saws faster, and the kite above has
  the edge. A bar at the crossing shows who is winning. Cut them: BO KATA! Two quickly: DOUBLE!
- Cut kites tumble down; touch one with your line to catch it (coins). The roofs below end your flight.
- Cut the kites the round asks for before the daylight (a bar) runs out.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever (LESSONS rule 0)
- Level 1: one rival, gentle, three cuts. More rivals (up to four), more cuts (up to six), rivals that hunt you more, pull
  harder and turn better; gusts from level 6; every fifth round the champion flies (one more rival, sharper line). Six skies.
- Three losses: more daylight and gentler rivals (help).

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves (BLIND in part2): `h` thumb down, `r` thumb up, `t` a quick tap.
- A blind thumb dives into the roofs (a dive is quicker than a climb), loses crossings from below, and a kite parked at the
  end of its line or the top of the sky cannot cut at all.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1. LET GO TO TURN, HOLD TO GO: fly through 4 rings.
2. CROSS AND CUT: cut a practice kite that hangs still.
3. PULL HARDER: cut a rival that fights back.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK: 3 to 5 principles, the sound rules)
- P3 Honest near-miss: the bar at a crossing shows exactly how close each cut was.
- P18 Combos and in-run streaks: DOUBLE, TRIPLE... cuts in a row.
- P7 Hit-stop and P8 shake: the snap of a cut, sparks at a crossing, the jolt when yours goes.
- P26 Variety with rhythm: six skies (a night festival with lanterns), gusts, champions.
- Sound: wind that rises with gusts, the spool's whirr as you pull, the rasp of sawing lines that rises as the cut comes, a
  snap and a call a step higher for each cut in a row (S5), a dhol pattern that gets busier when lines cross.

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1 Hold to pull, let go to turn: the arrow shows where a pull goes.
- L2 The kite answers at once; the line tightens and sags; sparks at a crossing.
- L3 The bar at a crossing; every loss says why (cut by whom, into the roofs, the daylight).
- L4 CUT n/m and the daylight bar on screen; the next round; the next kite or upgrade.
- L5 Daylight to spare (your best per round), catches, runs of cuts.
- L6 More rivals, gusts, champions, night.
- L7 Attack or wait for them to come; from above or now; catch a falling kite or chase the next.
- L8 FLY AGAIN is one tap.
- L9 Kites (won with stars), rank, coins and upgrades (sharp manja, strong line, light kite, big spool) stay.
- L10 Your best daylight to spare on each round.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
Two lines crossed, sparks flying, the bar filling, then BO KATA as the rival kite tumbles away.
