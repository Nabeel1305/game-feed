# 82. Dress Designer

## The idea (his words, then mine)
His words (20 Nov 2026, set 14 list): "Dress designer."

Mine: a calm design game in 3D. A client walks into your studio with a brief (a garden party, a gala, a beach day, a disco,
a prom, the office, a festival, winter), a few wishes on a card, and sometimes one thing she will NOT wear. You design the
dress part by part on a model turning on a platform: the dress changes the moment you tap. Then the lights go down, she walks
the runway to the photographers' flashes, and three judges give stars: THE CLIENT (were her wishes met?), STYLE (does
anything clash?) and CRAFT (are the finishing touches where they belong?). 3D because the dress on a turning model, its
fabric catching the light, and the runway walk are the whole pleasure; the controls stay flat, big buttons.

## Research (20 Nov 2026; I cannot play these myself, so I studied store pages, reviews and guides)
- Covet Fashion: themed styling challenges with a brief, judged by other players' votes; the brief is the hook. (store
  page, reviews)
- JoJo's Fashion Show: dress each model to match her motif, judged on a runway with up to five stars; three of five to pass.
  The runway judging is what people remember. (reviews, guides)
- Dress To Impress (Roblox): a theme, a timer, then a runway and votes; the walk and the reveal are the payoff. (guides)
- What reviewers complain about in dress-up games: they all look alike, the loop is short and there is no reason to come
  back, runway animations are stiff, and the scoring feels random.
- What I took: a brief with clear wishes (Covet), judges with stars and a pass mark (JoJo), the runway reveal (Dress To
  Impress); and against the complaints: judges who say exactly why (no random scores), new parts and rules level by level,
  and studios, rank and upgrades that stay.

## How it plays (one screen, one thumb)
- The brief card at the top: her name and occasion, each wish with a tick when the dress meets it, and a red ✗ for the thing
  she will not wear.
- One part at a time at the bottom (big buttons): Cut, Length, Sleeves, Fabric, Colour (16 swatches), Pattern, Finishing
  touch. A tap dresses the model at once. NEXT moves on; the last NEXT says TO THE SHOW.
- Finishing touch: tap a trim (bow, belt, lace, pearls, flowers), then tap the glowing ring on the dress where it goes
  (neck, waist or hem). Up to two.
- From level 8 one wish is hidden: ❓ ASK HER reveals it.
- The client waits on each part (30 s, falling to 12 s far on), then says "Lovely, next?".
- The show: a six-second walk down the runway, flashes, music; then the three judges' stars. Every judge needs three stars
  (three and a half from level 15). The end card lists every miss in words: "She wanted velvet (fabric)", "Sequins with a
  pattern: too busy", "Belt at the neck? It belongs at the waist" (his rule: mistakes explained so you see how to improve).

## His rules for every game (20 Nov 2026), kept
- Big touch controls that never cover the action: the option panel sits under the model; the dress stays in view.
- Fun within ten seconds: the first tap changes the dress.
- Mechanics one at a time: colour, length, pattern, fabric first; sleeves at 2; trims at 3; the cut at 4; the NO at 6; the
  hidden wish at 8.
- Autosave (level, stars, coins, studio, settings), pause anywhere, DESIGN AGAIN in one tap.
- Separate Music, Sound effects and Vibration (⚙ Settings, on home and in pause; vibration strength too).
- Original clients, places and studios; no real brands.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever (LESSONS rule 0)
- Level 1 asks for four parts, each wish naming one exact colour; new parts as above. Wishes rise to all seven, star clients
  every fifth level (one more wish), early on one miss is forgiven (levels 12-19), the pass mark rises at 15, the client's
  patience shortens. Six places in turn (Little Studio, City Atelier, Fashion Week, Seaside Show, Grand Gala...). Three losses
  in a row: kinder judges (help).

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves (BLIND in part2): `t` a tap on any option, `s` a tap on any ring, `n` NEXT.
- The dress starts on options that do not meet her wishes, so skipping through loses; random picks miss wishes, clash, and
  put trims in the wrong place. Blind strings won 1 of 5,696 games; random moves 0 of 480.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1. DESIGN: three wishes, tap to meet each, NEXT.
2. STYLE: a disco brief; sequins with a pattern clash (the lesson shows it).
3. FINISHING TOUCH: pick a trim, tap its ring.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK: 3 to 5 principles, the sound rules)
- P6 Every input answers back: the dress changes the instant you tap, a fabric swish (a glitter for sequins), a tick when a
  wish is met.
- P12 Celebrations scaled: the runway, flashes, the judges' stars each a note higher, SHOWSTOPPER for three stars, a tip.
- P3 Honest near-miss: every lost star is named in words, from the real judging ("She wanted velvet").
- P13 Visible personal best: your best average stars on each level.
- Sound: a soft plucked studio loop (music switch), a runway beat with camera shutters at the show, a drum roll, stars
  climbing the scale (S5).

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1 The card says what she wants; the panel says which part.
- L2 One tap, the dress changes.
- L3 Rules you can learn (clashes) and judges who explain.
- L4 The ticks on the card; the next client; the next studio.
- L5 Judges' stars and your best per level.
- L6 New parts, the NO, the hidden wish, star clients, six places.
- L7 Upgrades (runway lights, star model, fine fabrics, fame).
- L8 DESIGN AGAIN is one tap.
- L9 Studios (won with stars), rank, coins.
- L10 Your best average to beat.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
The model walking the runway in a sequinned gown to the flashes, then the judges' stars.
