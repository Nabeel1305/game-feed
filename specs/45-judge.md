# 45. The Bench

## The idea (his, 18 Nov 2026, set 8)
"You're a judge hearing thousands of varied cases, including morally complex situations, simple offences and grave crimes.
You write your own sentences instead of choosing preset options, and an AI interprets them. An optional judicial council
scores you after every five sentences, determining whether you progress to the next level. You can switch the council off
and sentence freely. Choose normal/PG or 18+ mode, with the latter including disturbing cases such as murder."
2D on purpose: it is papers, words and faces.

## How it plays (one thumb and the keyboard)
A case file: R v name, the offence, age and job, how they pleaded, what they did, the things that make it worse (⚠) and the
things in their favour (✓), the victim's words, and the law's maximum (and the victim's loss, and whether a driving ban is
usual). You type the sentence in your own words and tap PASS SENTENCE. The clerk reads it back in court language (Claude
reads it when the page can ask Claude; offline, in the phone app, the court's own reader understands plain English:
numbers and number words, years/months/weeks, prison, suspended, hours of unpaid work, fines, compensation, probation,
curfew, a driving ban, treatment, a discharge, life with a minimum term). Confirm, or Change. The gavel; the gallery reacts
(nods, gasps at a harsh one, boos at a soft one): a hint, not the mark.

After five sentences the judicial council marks each: Lady Justice Hale (the scholar), Judge Stern (the hawk) and Judge
Mercer (the dove) each judge proportion (against the usual sentence for that offence moved by each factor), lawfulness
(never above the maximum; murder is life; only two years or less may be suspended; cruel punishments are unlawful),
putting it right (compensation for a loss, about the size of the loss; a driving ban for driving offences and not for
others; treatment for an addiction), and consistency with your own earlier sentences for like cases. Their average
against the pass mark decides the level. Their reasons are on the report.

Council OFF (home card): free bench, cases for ever, no marks. 18+ ON (home card, asks you are 18+): murder, manslaughter,
a mercy killing, armed robbery, kidnapping, drug trafficking, wounding, death by dangerous driving, arson, a revenge
attack, all told without gore.

## Cases
29 offences (19 normal, 10 grave), 9 things that make it worse and 10 in their favour, 30 first names x 25 surnames x 15
jobs x shops x places x stories: thousands of cases. Hard cases (stealing food and insulin, a protest, a whistleblower, a
mercy killing, a revenge attack) have a wide fair range. Repeat offences come back (consistency).

## Levels
Pass mark from 66 rising to 86; more factors per case; more hard cases; more repeats of earlier offences. Four courts
(Magistrates', County, Crown, the Old Bailey). Help after three losses: a kinder pass mark.

## Systems
Ranks from Magistrate to Lord Chief Justice; the Robing Room (Senior clerk: the usual range for a first offender on the
file; Sentencing guidelines: each factor's weight; a finer robe); your record of sentences kept from session to session.

## Spamming cannot win
- Blind moves: `w` (a sentence of a random kind and size, with or without compensation, a ban, treatment), `x` (words that
  are not a sentence).
- Random sentences miss the range, add bans to thefts and compensation where nobody lost money: blind 0 of 768 and 480 in
  my runs. Ordinary robots (who weigh the case by eye with an error) win the first levels every time.

## Lessons
1. PASS SENTENCE: three fair sentences in a row for shoplifting. 2. WEIGH IT UP: three bike thefts with different people.
3. PUT IT RIGHT: three with compensation. Skip tutorial on every lesson; Tutorial on home and pause.

## Engagement factors chosen
P3 honest feedback (the clerk's reading, the gallery, the council's reasons); P14 the near goal (case k of 5, the pass
mark); P5 calm pacing (no clock); P22 the record that grows; P7 moral tension (hard cases).

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1: read, write, pass: everyone knows what a sentence is.
- L2: the gavel, the gallery's gasp, the council's seal.
- L3: the council says exactly why; the law's maximum is on the file.
- L4: five cases to the council, the pass mark on the screen.
- L5: your best average mark on each level.
- L6: 29 offences, hard cases, 18+ mode, four courts.
- L7: every sentence is a choice: prison or community, compensation, a ban, mercy.
- L8: Try again is one tap and the next case is on the file.
- L9: your rank, your record, your robing room.
- L10: your best mark on the home card.
