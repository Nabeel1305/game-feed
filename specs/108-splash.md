# 108. Splash Course (his idea 32, set 20)

## The idea
His words: a colourful 3D obstacle course above water: jumping, balancing, dodging moving obstacles, reaching the finish as
fast as possible, with funny falls and quick retries; five obstacles adapted from the show (giant balls, the sweeper, the
punch wall, the treadmill, the rotating cross). Intensity 4, two thumbs, played sideways.

**3D:** yes. Judging a jump onto a ball, an arm coming round, a glove about to punch all need depth, and the splash is the joke.

## Research
I cannot play other games myself, so I read reviews and guides of the phone games closest to it:
- **Wipeout and Wipeout 2** (Activision; Gamezebo, Common Sense Media, 148Apps, Phandroid): the left thumb moves, the right
  jumps or ducks; the Big Balls are the star; Wipeout 2 added a ghost rival racing your best time, which players liked, but
  reviewers disliked that any touch from any obstacle meant a long checkpoint restart, and that the ragdoll falls were taken
  out. Kept: the ghost of your best, the jump/duck split, funny tumbling falls. Changed: back at the start of that obstacle in
  under two seconds, one second on the clock; a fall always has a visible cause.
- **Stumble Guys** (Geek Girl Authority, LevelWinner, Kotaku): a drag stick bottom left, a jump button bottom right, short
  rounds and an instant next try. Its common complaint is loose controls that walk you off ledges by accident and a camera
  that fights you. So: the stick turns with the camera, the camera only changes angle while you stand on a safe platform,
  the punch-wall ledge damps sideways movement so a sloppy thumb does not walk you off, and a hold on JUMP crouches you in
  place (you cannot run off an edge while you charge a jump).
- **Fall Guys** (comparisons): readable, learnable obstacle patterns; the comedy is the physics, never the controls.

## How it plays (two thumbs, sideways)
- **Left thumb: a floating stick.** Up the screen is the way the camera looks (forward on most obstacles; on the punch wall,
  seen from the side, right on the screen is forward).
- **JUMP (big, bottom right): hold, then let go.** Holding crouches you and fills a power ring round the button; a ring on
  the water shows where you will land (green: a safe landing; yellow: the edge of a ball; red: water). No stick: straight up.
- **DUCK (smaller, beside it):** while held you crouch under high bars and arms.
- **Falls:** a splash, a tumble, the crowd's "ooh"; back at the start of that obstacle within two seconds, a second on the clock.
- **Win:** reach the finish platform inside the qualifying time; gold, silver or bronze for the time; your best run races you
  as a ghost (and the HUD says how far ahead or behind it you are).

## The five obstacles, point by point
1. **Big Bounce Balls:** four giant balls between platforms, alternating left and right. They squash under you and spring back;
   land near the middle and you stay (a clean landing); near the edge you slide, and a push back towards the middle saves you
   for a moment. Later: wider gaps, different heights, smaller balls, balls that sway sideways.
2. **The Sweeper:** jump onto a round platform; a padded arm circles round; jump it as it comes (a steady rhythm; from level 10
   a red arm means a faster round, shown the whole way round; from level 12 a second, high arm that needs DUCK, half a turn
   apart so there is room for both). Survive the set number of sweeps and the gate opens.
3. **Punch Wall:** a narrow ledge along a padded wall, seen from the side. Each glove's lamp flashes and the glove pulls back
   before it punches; stop in the gaps and go after a punch. The rhythm is fixed per level, and set so that walking straight
   through always gets punched: you have to watch and wait.
4. **The Treadmill:** a conveyor carrying you back towards the pool; run forwards, steer round padded barriers, jump low
   hurdles, duck high bars; lights flash before a short speed-up. Hold the stick to run (no repeated tapping).
5. **Spinning Cross:** a cross-shaped walkway turning between the platforms; watch, jump on as an arm comes by, it carries you
   round, walk to the end of the arm that will face the exit, and jump off. From level 10 a red bar sweeps the other way: jump it.

## His other directions
- Environment: a sunny water arena, bright padded equipment, sparkling pools (real reflecting water), stands of spectators,
  beach umbrellas, a finish arch with a chequered line. Kept.
- Controls: left stick, large JUMP, smaller DUCK, the same all the way through. Kept.
- Camera: a clear angle for each obstacle (behind and above for the balls and the treadmill, high for the sweeper and the cross,
  from the side for the punch wall), changing smoothly only while you are on a safe platform; the landing ring always shows
  where you will land. Kept.
- Course structure: levels 1-5 teach one obstacle each (the first three with a practice run, no clock); then courses of two to
  four obstacles, and every fifth level the full five-obstacle course (about one and a half to two minutes). Kept.
- Funny falls: tumbling, big splashes, the crowd jumping up; precise control while standing. Kept.
- Quick recovery: under two seconds, one second's penalty, never the whole course. Kept.
- Fair difficulty: every hazard has a fixed, learnable rhythm per level and a visible cause (lamps, red arm, warning lights).
- Scoring: medals for time; extra coins for no splash and for clean landings; the ghost of your best. Kept.
- Sound and music: an upbeat tune (Music switch), squeaky balls, boings, mechanical whooshes, punches, the belt's rumble,
  splashes and the crowd. Kept.
- Vibration: light pulses for jumps and landings, a stronger one for being hit, none for losing; can be turned off. Kept.
- Progression: new places (Sunny Lagoon, Palm Bay, Sunset Pools, Storm Cove), outfits unlocked with stars, upgrades (grippy
  socks, spring soles, big lungs), harder combinations. Kept.
- Relaxed mode: "Relaxed practice" on the home card: the same course with no qualifying time and endless tries. Kept.
- Visual identity: its own padded colours and courses; no show branding. Kept.

## Engagement factors
P3 honest near-miss (the landing ring, the HUD's lead over your ghost), P4 fair, readable failure (a hint for every splash:
what got you and what to do), P13 visible personal best (the ghost and your best time on the home card), P17 rare harmless
surprises (the tumbles and the crowd's reactions), P23 instant start and restart (back on the obstacle in two seconds).
- L1 understanding: run, jump onto the next thing, do not fall in. L2 controls: one stick, one hold-and-release button.
- L3 fair: fixed rhythms, visible causes. L4 near goal: the next obstacle and the progress bar. L5 improvement: medals, ghost.
- L6 variety: five obstacles that each ask something different. L7 choices: a big jump or a safe one; wait for a gap or rush.
- L8 retry within a second or two. L9 yours: outfits, upgrades, best times. L10 the ghost of your best.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
Blind moves: `f` push forward, `s` push anywhere, `o` let go, `j` tap JUMP, `h` hold JUMP a while, `d` hold DUCK a while.
The balls alternate left and right so a straight jump lands on an edge; the most a jump can carry cannot skip a ball; the
punch wall always catches a steady walker; the treadmill's barriers always cover the middle. Measured: every blind string of
up to four moves (46,528 games) won 13 in the node check; random moves 0 of 480; the lessons are not finished by 90 s of
blind tapping.

## Levels
Balls (1), Sweeper (2), Punch Wall (3), Treadmill (4), Spinning Cross (5), then mixes. Qualifying time from 1.9 × par down to
1.15 × par; gold at par. Help after three losses: slower hazards and more time.
