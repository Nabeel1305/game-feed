# 20. Potter's Wheel

## The idea (mine, for the calm side of set 3)
A potter's corner of an old workshop in 3D (a brick wall, a window onto the yard with the level's weather and light, shelves
of glazed pots, a lamp). A lump of wet clay spins on the wheel; the order's outline is dotted on both sides. Very calm (1),
one thumb. 3D because the spinning, shining clay taking shape is the whole pleasure.

## How it plays (one thumb: your fingertip on the clay)
- Touch the side of the spinning clay and move slowly: the clay follows your fingertip in or out at that height (and a little
  above and below). Too far from the clay and your finger does nothing; quick, hard pushes make it wobble and it can collapse.
- The match bar climbs as the shape nears the outline. Tap FIRE when it passes the mark: the pot is glazed and goes to the
  shelf. Fire too early and it cracks in the kiln.
- From level 6 the clay dries: shape it before the blue bar runs out. Three ruined pots and the day is over.

## Levels
Simple outlines first (three control points), up to six later, curving more; the mark rises from 82% to 89%; more pots a day;
the clay dries sooner. Robots: an ordinary player wins all of 1 to 20 and 6 in 10 of level 40.

## Spamming cannot win
Blind moves: a slow stroke in or out at some height, a stroke up or down the side, fire. 0 of 25,920 strings; random 0 of 480.
First found: a blind FIRE "won" a one-pot level by cracking (the day counted pots made, not pots fired). It now counts fired pots.

## Lessons
1: the clay follows your finger; match and fire. 2: in and out (a curve). 3: two orders, slow hands.

## Engagement factors chosen
P6 every touch answers (the clay moves under the finger, slip flies, a wet whisper), P13 best match per level, P20 a gentle
palette (terracotta and lamplight), P21 tidy satisfaction (the shape settling onto the line), P30 the kiln's ting.
