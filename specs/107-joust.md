# 107. Joust (his idea 26, set 20)

## The idea
His words: compete in jousting tournaments; charge an opponent on horseback, aim your lance and land a powerful hit while
staying in the saddle. Intensity 4, two thumbs, played sideways.

**3D:** yes. The lists are the game: the long barrier, the rival thundering towards you, the crowd, the castle. The crash
needs depth (the rival growing in front of you) to read.

## Research
I cannot play other games myself, so I studied reviews and store pages of the best phone jousting games:
- **Shake Spears!** (148Apps, Pocket Gamer): reviewers called its controls the most complete jousting controls on a phone:
  one thumb slides on the left for the shield, the other on the right for the lance. That split is exactly his direction
  (left lean, right aim), so I kept it. Pocket Gamer also found its shield side "rarely required" and the whole thing a
  rock-paper-scissors lottery: so here the lean matters (it moves their hit, at a cost) and nothing is a coin toss.
- **Rival Knights** (Gameloft; AppSpy, Droid Gamers, Phone Arena): the run-up is a drag race; near the crash the game goes into
  slow motion so you can guide the lance onto a weak spot that changes each round; hits end in big falls. Reviews loved the
  crash and the slow motion, and disliked energy timers, pay-to-win and repetition. Kept: a target that moves each pass (their
  shield sits differently every pass), an optional slow-motion crash. Left out: energy, paid power.
- **Joust Legend** (Pocket Gamer, App Store): motion-captured with a real champion jouster; you lower the lance, then position
  it, and timing depends on the lance's weight and the horse's speed. Players' biggest complaint: it never explained the
  scoring or that unhorsing was possible. So here the scoring is on the home card before the first match, the lessons teach
  each part, and unhorsing happens and is shown.
- **Knight Lancer** (Pocket Gamer): a simple physics joust whose whole aim is to unseat the opponent; players praised the
  sound and art, and found the shield position fiddly. So the seat (balance) is the drama here, and the lean is one plain
  left/right slide.

## How it plays (two thumbs, sideways)
- The horse gallops by itself; each pass lasts about four seconds from the end of the lists to the crash.
- **Right thumb: drag to aim.** The lance starts raised; drag to lower it and move the tip. The aim stays where you leave it.
  The tip **sways with the gallop in a steady figure of eight** (the same rhythm every pass at a level), so you aim where the
  tip *will be* at the crash. At the easier levels its path ahead and a yellow ring (where it will be at the crash) are drawn.
- **Left thumb: lean.** A floating stick, left or right. Leaning late (after their aim is set) moves your shield out of their
  line; leaning far at the crash costs you your seat, and leaning shifts your own tip a little.
- **BRACE (big button, bottom right): hold it just before the crash.** It glows in the last half second. Braced on time: your
  hit counts, a centre hit breaks your lance (2 points) and their hit pushes you back much less. Braced too early: your arm
  tires and the tip sways wider. Not braced: your lance skids off for no point, and their hit rocks you.
- **Scoring:** shield hit 1, a braced centre hit (the lance breaks) 2; unhorse them and you win outright. Three passes, and
  extra passes if it is level. Shown on the home card before the first match.
- **Seat (balance):** every hit pushes the rider back; riders settle between passes (some quicker than others); an empty seat
  is a fall: they topple off (or you do), non-graphic.
- After each crash a card shows **where both lances struck** (your tip on their shield, theirs on yours) and whether you were
  braced on time, early or not at all.

## His directions, point by point
- View: 3D lists, long wooden barrier, banners, stands of spectators, a castle beyond; camera behind and above you. Kept.
- Controls: horse runs by itself, left drag leans, right drag aims, a large brace button that matters just before contact. Kept.
- Basic round: start at opposite ends, charge, strike, turn for another pass if nobody is unseated. Kept (passes).
- Aiming: tip clearly visible (a ring and cross), sways in a consistent learnable rhythm, aim help that starts generous
  (pulls the tip in when near) and shrinks with the levels. Kept.
- Timing: rewarded for lowering the lance and bracing at the right moment; early bracing makes aiming harder (tired arm, wider
  sway), late leaves you exposed (no point, bigger knock). Kept.
- Impact: sharp wooden crack, splinters, camera jolt, a strong short vibration; settings to turn camera shake and vibration
  off (Settings: Vibration, Vibration strength, Camera shake). Kept.
- Balance: riders rock back, slip sideways and recover after weaker hits; a strong, well-placed strike unhorses. Kept.
- Scoring: points for clean shield hits and lance breaks, outright victory for unseating; shown before the first match. Kept.
- Rival variety: six rivals with habits (aims true but recovers slowly, braces like a wall, shifts late, ...); the late
  shifter's head dips towards the side they will shift to (the clue in their posture). Kept.
- Progression: training targets first (levels 1-2), then single rivals in tournament rounds (first round, quarter-final,
  semi-final, final, again with better knights); better rival timing, aim and sturdiness as you go. Kept.
- Sound and music: pounding hoofbeats that quicken, rattling armour, the crowd's murmur before the crash and a roar or groan
  after, a herald's horn; a stately tune between passes (its own Music switch). Kept.
- Replay appeal: instant rematch (REMATCH on the end card, a tap), an optional slow-motion crash (Settings), cosmetic colours
  for your armour, shield, plume and horse cloth (six heraldic sets), upgrades (balanced lance, high saddle, steady horse).
  Honest note: the slow motion plays live, at the crash, rather than as a replay afterwards.
- Fair failure: the card shows where each lance struck and why a rider fell; falls are a topple, not graphic; rematch at once.

## Engagement factors
P4 fair, readable failure (the card of where both lances struck, and why), P5 teach by doing (three short lessons, one
skill each), P8 shake and permanence (the jolt, splinters, a broken lance stays broken for the pass; the shake can be turned
off), P11 haptics (a short heavy pulse for a lance break, never for a loss), P26 variety with rhythm (six rivals with habits,
tournament rounds, four venues).
- L1 immediate understanding: the tip, the shield, the glowing BRACE. L2 satisfying controls: drag aims, the aim stays.
- L3 fair challenge: a learnable rhythm, a visible tell. L4 near goal: the points and the pass on screen.
- L5 visible improvement: the hit card, best coins, rank. L6 variety that changes decisions: rival habits.
- L7 choices: lean late or stay square; brace early (safe) or on time (2 points). L8 retry in a second: REMATCH.
- L9 yours: your colours and upgrades. L10 a ghost of your best: your best coins on the home card.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
Blind moves: `a` aim anywhere, `c` aim at the middle, `l` lean anywhere, `b` hold BRACE a while, `r` let go. Aiming at the
middle no longer scores by luck: their shield sits differently every pass, the sway moves the tip, an unbraced lance skids off,
and wild leaning costs your seat and dodges only a little. Measured: every blind string, 25,920 games, won 4; random moves 0
of 480; lesson 1 (three near the middle in a row) is not finished by 90 s of blind tapping.

## Levels
1-2 training at the target (break three lances in five), 3 on: a rival each level (six in turn), tournament round names.
Sway 0.30 to 0.42, rhythm 1.6 to 2.3 a second, aim help 0.45 to 0.1, rival skill 0.6 to 1.2, rival sturdiness 1 to 0.6.
Help after three losses: more aim help, a weaker rival.
