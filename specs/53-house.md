# 53. Homemaker

## The idea (his, 19 Nov 2026, set 9)
"Build increasingly complex houses through successive levels. Early levels could involve only the exterior, followed by
interiors and eventually entire houses. Building should remain simple to control while allowing large, intricate homes...
Early builds should be quick; later builds can take longer." With his tips: effortless placing, snapping, rotating and undo;
small tasks first; clear requirements with creative freedom; satisfying progress; small plots and unusual requirements; walk
through the finished house; autosave; revisit and improve earlier houses. 3D: you walk through what you built.

## How it plays (one thumb)
The plot from above at an angle (turn the view a quarter at a time; the roof can be hidden to see inside). Pick a piece in the
tray and tap or drag on the plot: WALL (snaps to the grid lines; drag for a run), WINDOW and DOOR (tap a wall to turn it into
one), FLOOR (drag over squares), furniture (bed, stove, table, sofa, bath, desk, plant: tap a square, tap again to turn it),
TREE (outside), ROOF (one tap roofs every closed house), ERASE, UNDO. A room is floor closed in by walls; what is in it makes it
a bedroom, a kitchen, a bathroom... The checklist ticks as you build; MOVE IN when it is all ticked, and the camera walks you
in through the front door and round every room. The crew has a number of jobs before the family arrives (every piece placed
or erased is a job; undo is free but the mistake already cost one): run out with the house not ready and the level is lost.

## Levels
A garden shed (floor, walls, a door), a cottage (windows, a roof), a furnished one-room home, a two-room cottage, then homes of
three to five rooms (living rooms from 5, bathrooms from 6, studies from 9), two front doors from 7, trees from 7, tight plots
from 8, and fewer spare jobs as the levels go on. Help: more jobs and a bigger piece budget.

## Systems
The checklist and wishes (a window in every room, plants, trees), the piece budget (stars), the jobs before the family
arrives, every house kept (open a level again to improve it; a house in progress is saved as you go), the walk-through, the
Hardware Store (brick walls, clay tiles, painted doors), ranks from Apprentice to City maker.

## Spamming cannot win
- Blind moves: `t` (pick a random tool), `p` (tap somewhere on the plot), `m` (MOVE IN), `u` (undo).
- A house is a closed set of walls round enough floor with doors, windows, rooms and a roof: blind 0 of 13,056, random 0 of 480.

## Lessons
1. FLOOR AND WALLS: a 4-square floor, walls all round, a door. 2. WINDOWS AND A ROOF (on a built shell). 3. FURNISH IT.

## Engagement factors chosen
P22 a collection that grows (every house you built, kept); P14 the near goal (the checklist ticking); P5 calm flow; P3 honest
feedback (what is still needed, in words); P10 sound (each piece its own knock, rising in a run).

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1: walls, a door, a roof: everyone knows a house.
- L2: pieces snap where you tap; drag for a run; undo is always there.
- L3: the checklist says exactly what is missing.
- L4: the checklist, the pieces against the budget, the jobs left.
- L5: your fewest pieces on each house.
- L6: more rooms and kinds of room, tight plots, wishes.
- L7: where to put each room, how to spend the jobs.
- L8: Try again is one tap; your build in progress is kept.
- L9: every house you built is yours to revisit; the Hardware Store.
- L10: the walk-through of your own house.

## The card clip
A wall run snapping round a floor, windows popping in, the roof dropping on, and the camera walking in through the front door.
