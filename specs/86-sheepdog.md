# 86. Sheepdog

## The idea (mine, for set 15)
You are the sheepdog. You don't command the sheep, you push them: they walk away from you, keep together, and panic and
scatter if you rush them. Gather the strays, drive the flock across the fields (round trees, over the bridge) and through
the pen's gate before sunset. Later: lambs that follow their mothers, a black ram who only moves for a bark, and at dusk a
fox that creeps up on any sheep left alone. 3D, from above and behind: the flock moving like water across a green field,
the light warming and fading as the day runs out, is the pleasure of it.

Why it balances set 15: Dress Designer (1, calm), Taxi Driver (3), Roach Run (4), Hoop Shot (2), Sheepdog (3); genres
fashion, driving, stealth, sport and now herding; one thumb (Roach Run is the two-thumb one).

## Research (20 Nov 2026; I cannot play these myself, so I studied reviews and store pages)
- Flock! (2009): herding with "fear"; reviewers praise how each animal reacts differently and that it teaches one property
  at a time; early levels "take a while" while you learn not to send them running the wrong way. (GameSpot, AV Club)
- Happy Flock (phones): you drag your finger and the collie follows it; reviewers call it very cute, but the time limits
  "rarely generous" and it needs patience. (Gamezebo)
- Sheepherds! (2025): sheep follow and influence each other; you position yourself and bark at the right moment; dashing
  at them splits the flock. Players say steering the flock feels "like steering water". (Steam, Ladies Gamers, GameGrin)
- Herdling (2025): "step to their left and they swing right"; some reviewers found the herding janky and hazards that never
  evolve; one wished for a more direct way to steer. (Nintendo Life, TechRadar, TheSixthAxis)
- Herding Adventures: "herding isn't about control, it's about pressure": too fast and they scatter. (Steam page)
- What I took: Happy Flock's control (the dog runs where your thumb is, the most direct way on a phone), with Sheepherds'
  bark and its splitting when you rush; Flock's one-new-thing-at-a-time; and against the complaints: generous early days,
  hazards that change (lambs, the ram, the river, the fox), and a "walking / running" readout so speed is understood.

## How it plays (one thumb)
- Hold on the field: the dog runs to the spot under your thumb (a ring marks it) and follows as you slide; the further the
  spot, the faster she runs. Let go: she lies down (and presses on the sheep less). A quick tap: BARK.
- Sheep move away from the dog (within about 9 m), keep together and in step; run at them and they panic and bolt.
- Lambs follow their mothers; the black ram ignores pressure unless the dog is right on him or barks; the river is crossed
  only by the bridge (pushed at the bank, sheep follow it to the bridge); trees are walked round.
- The fox (from level 5, at dusk) creeps up on the loneliest sheep and carries it off; run at it or bark to see it off.
- Pen the number needed before sunset; enough in, and the rest get four seconds of grace.

## His rules for every game (20 Nov 2026), kept
- No buttons over the field: the whole screen is the control.
- Fun within ten seconds: lesson 1 is three sheep and the pen right there.
- One thing at a time: push (1), gather (2), bark (3); lambs at 3, the river at 4, the fox at 5, the ram at 6.
- Mistakes explained: "TOO FAST! THEY SCATTER", the fox ringed and pointed at, arrows to strays off the screen.
- Autosave, pause, separate Music, Sound effects and Vibration (Settings); original farm and dogs.

## Levels
- Flocks from 5 to 16 (18 on every fifth field), spread wider and further from the pen; more trees; a shorter day. Six
  fields in turn (Home Meadow, River Farm, High Moor, Valley at Dusk, Misty Hills, Rainy Downs). Three losses: a longer day.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves (BLIND in part2): `d` the thumb down somewhere on the field (or let go), `b` a bark.
- Random rushing scatters the flock and the day runs out.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK: 3 to 5 principles, the sound rules)
- P6 Every input answers back: the ring under your thumb, the dog's run or crouch, the flock's flow.
- P3 Honest near-miss: the count and the sun bar; the end card says how many were in and why.
- P12 Celebrations scaled: a bleat and a chime a step higher for each sheep in, GOOD DOG, CHAMPION.
- P13 Visible personal best: best coins per field.
- Sound: a whistle to start, the bark, bleats (lambs high, the ram low), hooves when they scatter, the fox's cry, birdsong
  that stops at dusk; music (its own switch): a plucked folk tune over a drone that thins as the sun goes.

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1 The pen is up the field; the sheep are there; the dog is yours. L2 One thumb. L3 Strays come back if you go round them.
- L4 The count and the sun. L5 Seconds before sunset; scatters. L6 Lambs, the ram, the river, trees, the fox, six fields.
- L7 Gather first or drive now; upgrades (long legs, big bark, calm eye, good nose). L8 TRY AGAIN is one tap.
- L9 Eight dogs, rank, coins. L10 Best coins per field.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
The flock pouring over the bridge toward the pen in golden light, the dog crouched behind them.
