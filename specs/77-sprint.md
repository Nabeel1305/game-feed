# 77. Sprint

## The idea (his words, then mine)
His words (20 Nov 2026): "Running game like sprinting. You tap your 2 thumbs at the bottom of the phone, each side. You must
time the touches in order to run. You win or lose the race."

Mine: a real athletics meeting, in 3D. Two big pads at the bottom are your two feet. Each tap is a stride, and the next foot is
due when the last one lands, so the beat speeds up as you speed up: a ring closes on the pad that is due, and tapping as it
closes is PERFECT. Mashing trips you. Around that: the starter's gun (react fast, never early: a false start, then you are
out), hurdles (both thumbs together to jump), the dip at the line (both thumbs in the last metres), five events (60 m, 100 m,
60 m and 110 m hurdles, 200 m round the bend), heats, semi-finals and finals at six stadiums, the ghost of your best run, and
kit and upgrades that stay. 3D because the race is the stadium around you: the crowd, the lanes, the runners beside you.

## Research (20 Nov 2026; I cannot play these myself, so I studied reviews, guides and store pages)
- Sprint 100 and Track Sprinter: tap the left and right of the screen in turn; the wrong order makes you fall. (Google Play,
  App Store pages)
- Speed Stars (one of the best-loved running games on phones): rhythm matters more than tap speed; tap too fast and you trip,
  too slow and you never catch up. (App Store page, players' guides)
- Athletics Hero and the classic Track & Field: a reaction start (a boost under a tenth and a bit, a false start for going
  early), hurdles with a jump button, a dip at the line; several events with the same controls. (store pages, retro reviews)
- What I took: a visible beat (the closing ring) so rhythm can be learnt by eye as well as by ear; trips for mashing and for
  the same thumb twice; a real start (reaction time shown, a second false start disqualifies); both thumbs together as the one
  extra move (jump, dip), so the controls stay two pads; events that change what you must do with the same two thumbs.

## How it plays (one screen, two thumbs)
- Bottom left and bottom right: LEFT and RIGHT pads (a touch anywhere on that half counts). Tap the side whose ring is
  closing, as it closes: PERFECT (gold) drives you hardest and builds FLOW (up to 10, each a little more top speed); GOOD and
  EARLY push less; LATE lets you slow while you wait. Far too soon, or the same side twice: TRIP (speed lost, FLOW gone).
- The start: ON YOUR MARKS, SET (keep still), BANG. Your reaction is shown; under 0.15 s is a LIGHTNING START. Tap on SET, or
  under a tenth after the gun: FALSE START, everyone back; a second and you are out.
- Both thumbs together: jump a hurdle (a green mark on your lane shows where to take off), or dip at the line (the last few
  metres; too early slows you).
- Your place, the clock, the field down the track and how far you are ahead of or behind your best run (its ghost runs in your
  lane) are always on screen. The top 2 or 3 go through.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever (LESSONS rule 0)
- 1-2: 60 m heats; 3: 60 m hurdles; 4: 100 m semi; 5: the 100 m final. Then every five: 60 m, 100 m, 110 m hurdles, 200 m,
  the 100 m final. Six stadiums in turn (School Track, City Stadium, Night Lights, Rain Meet, Golden Games, World Final).
- The field gets faster with the level (slower still in the first six), finals a little faster again; the top 3 go through
  up to level 12, then the top 2. Three losses on a race: a slightly slower field (help).
- Tested: an ordinary player wins levels 1-20 nearly always and falls away by 40; a sloppy one wins level 1 about 70%; a sharp
  one still wins at 80.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1): the wrong move costs, more each time; the right move needs one read
- Blind moves (BLIND in part2): `l` left tap, `r` right tap, `b` both together.
- Tapping before the gun is a false start, and a second one disqualifies: a thumb that does not wait for the bang is out.
- A beat tapped blind cannot follow the stride, which quickens as you speed up: too fast trips you, too slow floats you.

## Lessons of the first three levels (each move, two or three times, before play)
1. LEFT, RIGHT, IN TIME: 10 strides in a row without tripping, on an empty track.
2. WAIT FOR THE GUN: two clean starts (a false start just starts the lesson's start again).
3. BOTH THUMBS: JUMP: clear 3 hurdles in a row.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK: 3 to 5 principles, the sound rules)
- P6 Every input answers back: each tap is a spike on the track, a flash on the pad and a word (PERFECT, GOOD, LATE, TRIP).
- P18 Combos and in-run streaks: FLOW, perfect after perfect, with pips, a cyan glow and a rising note under every perfect.
- P13 Visible personal best: your best time per event, and its ghost in your lane, ahead or behind in metres.
- P26 Variety with rhythm: five events, heats, semis, finals, six stadiums with their own weather and light.
- P12 Celebrations scaled: a reaction call, a photo finish in slow motion, the crowd's roar, medals and the results board.
- Sound: spikes on tartan (left and right a touch apart), perfect notes climbing the scale with FLOW (S5), the gun with its
  echo round the stands (S9), a hush on SET, the crowd rising down the straight and roaring at the line.

## Longevity (PLAYBOOK H)
- L1 Two pads, a closing ring: understood in one race.
- L2 The stride answers at once, with sound, flash and word; the runner's legs follow your beat.
- L3 Trips and false starts say exactly why ("TOO SOON", "WRONG FOOT", "Wait for the BANG").
- L4 Top 3 go through (on screen, with your place live); the final at every fifth race; the next upgrade.
- L5 Your reaction, your % perfect, your top speed and your time against your best, every race.
- L6 Five events change what you do: the gun, hurdles, the bend and the longer run of the 200 m, the dip.
- L7 Upgrades to choose between (spikes, blocks, rhythm, spring); when to dip.
- L8 RACE AGAIN is one tap; a race is 7 to 25 seconds.
- L9 Your kit colours (won with stars), rank, coins and upgrades stay.
- L10 The ghost of your best run in each event races you.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
The last 15 m of a 100 m final at the photo-finish camera: the field abreast, the dip, the crowd up.
