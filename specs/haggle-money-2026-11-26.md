# Haggle: the day's money (26 Nov 2026, version 0.3.0)

## His words (in the chat, 12:23)
"With the Haggle game, don't you think it would be better if every level you had a limited amount of money? ... and you had to make purchases
and make as much profit as you could, right, out of that amount of money that you had, wouldn't it be fun? Wouldn't it be funner? And the higher
you go, the kind of harder it gets and the more you learn, don't you think?"

Answer: yes. Before, he could buy everything that walked in; the only question was the price. With money that runs out, every purchase is also a
choice of what NOT to buy, and every pound haggled off buys something else.

## What it does
- **The purse.** The day is dealt in the morning as before. `G.cash0` = `purse` x what every good thing (real, and to be had for less than it
  fetches) would cost at its seller's lowest: 0.85 on level 1 falling to about 0.45. Never less than the cheapest good thing.
- **The target.** `G.best` = the profit of the good things that purse could buy, best margin first. Target = `keen` x best: 0.25 on level 1 rising to
  0.44. A day of thin margins asks for at least a seventh of the purse or a third of its best, whichever is less. Help (three losses): more money,
  lower target.
- **Spending.** A purchase comes out of the purse (the gold MONEY plate counts down; red at a fifth left; "−£50" under it on a deal).
- **Where his money ends.** A white post on the dial (YOUR MONEY £75), the dial dark red beyond it. Beyond it the OFFER button is red and says
  "£135: MORE THAN YOU HAVE"; pressed, it is a bluff and they walk out (it counts as one of the two walk-outs).
- **All of it.** The dial catches on the post. "OFFER £75: ALL OF IT" is taken at once if it is at least their lowest; if not they go elsewhere,
  with no walk-out.
- **Closing early.** When nothing worth buying that is still to come can be afforded: MONEY SPENT: CLOSING UP.
- **The end of the day.** "You had £880 · spent £640 · it sold for £1,010", profit against target, and "the best that money could have made today".
- **Three offers.** A deal below their price takes three offers: they counter the first two (coming down each time) and take the third if it is at
  least their lowest. Meeting their price makes the deal at any moment.
- **Lessons** are taught without the purse (they are about bargaining). Nothing new tells him what to do outside the tutorial: the plate, the post and
  the red button are names and states.
