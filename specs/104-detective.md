# 104. Detective (his idea 33, 21 Nov 2026)

## The idea (his words, then mine)
His: solve mysteries: investigate scenes, collect evidence, question people and connect clues; cases more complex as you go.
Mine: every case is generated from its truth first (who, how, when, and where everyone really was), then the clues that
follow from it are placed on the furniture of the rooms, some in plain sight, some only on the back or underside. One
suspect lies to hide the crime; from level 3 another lies to hide an unrelated secret. Accuse with who, how, when and the
two clues that prove it. Intensity 1, one thumb, upright.

**3D:** yes for the rooms and the close-ups (his direction: elevated 3D locations and close-ups you turn); the case itself
(people, notebook, accusation) is a paper panel below, because lists and choices read better flat.

## How it plays (one thumb, upright)
- **The room** (top half), seen from a corner above: every object you have not looked at has a gold ring. **Tap** one and it
  comes up close: **drag** to turn it, **pinch** to zoom, **tap** what you notice (a hidden mark is a small dark smudge on one
  side). Room buttons along the top; the crime room has a ✦.
- **Case tab:** the briefing, the facts you have proven, how much of each room you have searched.
- **People:** tap a person, ask "Where were you this evening?" and "What do you know about ...?"; **Show a clue**: their own
  clue at the scene cracks a liar ("story cracked", a fact); a secret clue makes someone own up to their secret, which
  opens "Who can vouch for that?"; a clue about someone else costs a little patience (three, then they stop talking).
- **Notebook:** facts ✓, clues, and what everyone said, saved as you go. **Link** a clue to a person and say what it shows
  (puts them at the scene, shows how, gives a motive, clears them): a link the evidence supports becomes a fact ✓, one it does
  not stays "your theory: not proven".
- **Accuse:** who, how (four ways), when (five times), and pick the two clues that prove it (one that puts them at the scene,
  one that shows how). A wrong accusation is explained through the evidence ("The guest book: Ada in the Bar at 9 pm. Ada
  could not have been in the Study.") and costs a try; two wrong and the case goes cold (and you are shown the answer).
- **Hints 💡:** first the question still open, then the room to look in, then the object itself (glowing).
- **Quick case:** a case from any level you have reached, without lessons.

## Cases and progression
- Kinds: thefts (always first), sabotage, fraud, disappearances (pets, a professor), locked rooms; each has four ways it
  could have been done, each leaving its own trace and its own clue tying a person to it.
- Places: a country house, a grand hotel, a museum, a night train, an office.
- Level 1: one room, three suspects, a missing object (his "first mystery"). More suspects (to six), more rooms (to four),
  a secret-keeper from level 3 and two from 12, and more clues hidden on the far side of things as you go.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves: `t` tap somewhere (on the room, or on an object being examined), `r` turn the object, `g` another room,
  `q` a random question, `p` show a random clue to a random person, `a` accuse at random.
- Why they lose: an accusation needs four parts right at once (who x how x when x two clues out of all those found: about
  1 in 1,600 at level 1), and two wrong ones end the case.
- Proved: 0 of 864 blind strings, 0 of 120 random.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1. **LOOK CLOSELY:** find three hidden marks (turn objects over, tap what you notice).
2. **CHALLENGE A STORY:** show two people a clue about them (a clue about someone else starts the count again).
3. **PROVE IT:** a full accusation, with everything already in the notebook.

## Research (I cannot play other games; I read about them)
- **Return of the Obra Dinn** (G2A, Gaming.net, GamesRadar): pure deduction; solving feels like you solved it, not like the
  game handed it over after enough clicking; small details (a uniform, a place at a table) separate people
  (https://www.g2a.com/news/features/10-best-detective-games-mystery-solving-adventures/ ,
  https://www.gaming.net/best-detective-games-with-deduction-systems/). So: the accusation must be reasoned (four parts and
  two proving clues), and clicking everything does not win.
- **The Case of the Golden Idol** (same lists): a clue-connection puzzle. So: the notebook links clue to person with a
  meaning, and only supported links become facts.
- **Shadows of Doubt** (Gaming.net): a procedural world makes trails out of everyday traces; a receipt leads somewhere next.
  So: cases are generated from their truth, and every clue is an everyday object in the right room.
- **Criminal Case and Cluedo** (FinalBoss round-up): hidden-object scene searching on phones, and Cluedo's clear finish line:
  who, how and where (https://finalboss.io/12-best-mobile-detective-and-mystery-games-for-android-and-ios). So: a scene search
  with big, clear objects (not pixel-hunting), and a who/how/when accusation.
- **Mobile detective lists** (FinalBoss): too many phone mysteries let you solve privately and hand you a cutscene; deduction
  needs a dramatic release, and contradiction-hunting is its own kind
  (https://finalboss.io/12-best-mobile-detective-games-from-pocketgamers-android-and-ios-picks). So: showing a liar their own
  clue is the dramatic moment (a low note, a flash, "story cracked"), and the music lifts.

## His directions, point by point
- View: 3D rooms from an elevated angle, close-ups; homes, hotels, museums, trains, offices; clues that fit the room. Kept.
- Controls: tap to move or interact, drag to rotate, pinch to zoom; big objects. Kept.
- Basic case: briefing, investigate, interview, connect, conclude with supporting clues. Kept.
- First mystery: a missing object, three suspects, a small location; taught by discoveries. Kept.
- Finding clues: observation (a mark on the far side; a personal item where its owner says they never were). Kept.
- Examining objects: turning reveals clues or rules things out (every object says what it holds). Kept.
- Interviews: clear questions, follow-ups unlocked by evidence, present a clue to challenge a statement. Kept.
- Deduction board: automatic notebook, links with a meaning, facts vs theories. Kept (tap to link, easier than dragging).
- Interesting suspects: a secret-keeper who lies for another reason. Kept.
- Fair solutions: the truth is made first, and the clues follow from it; every needed clue exists. Kept.
- Variety: thefts, disappearances, sabotage, fraud, locked rooms. Kept.
- Hints: question, place, then the clue. Kept.
- Final accusation: what happened, who, which evidence. Kept.
- Mistakes: theories can be changed; wrong accusations explained through the evidence. Kept.
- Sound: soft mystery music that lifts on a contradiction, each place's sound, footsteps, discovery chimes. Kept.
- Progression: short cases first, then more rooms, more people, secrets; progress saved as you go. Kept.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK)
P4 fair, readable failure (wrong accusations explained by evidence), P5 teach by doing (three lessons), P12 celebrations
scaled (a chime for a clue, more for a key clue, a flash and a low note for a cracked story), P15 nested goals (each room,
each person, the accusation, the next case). Sound rules S1-S11. Longevity L1-L10: tap an object and you are investigating;
taps, a drag and a pinch; every wrong answer says why; the rooms show how much is left; facts build up; new places, kinds
and secret-keepers; who to talk to, what to show; a new case in a tap; coats by stars and kit (lens, notebook, nerve);
best per level.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
Showing the culprit their own glove from the scene: the low note, the flash, "That... I must have dropped it earlier."
