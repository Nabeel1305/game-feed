# Police Chase, third version (0.3.0): the foot chase and the arrest, from his second review

## His words (23 Nov 2026, 07:59, in the chat)
"Another fix for police chase. So when they're running, you have to make it clear how do you arrest them. Like you catch them, but
then there should be a button for arrest this person, and maybe you make it fun. You have to tap quicker than they're resisting, so
they get away. And also, when you arrest someone, the graphics are kind of messed up. For when you actually catch him and you arrest
him, you can check that the graphics are messed up. And in the same way, the person when running should also be able to turn
corners, and I can turn corners, and you make the controls for that."

## What each point becomes
| His point | What was there | What it is now |
|---|---|---|
| "make it clear how do you arrest them... a button for arrest this person" | a tap anywhere on the screen, with only a line of small text saying so | an **ARREST** button at the bottom of the screen for the whole foot chase: dim, with how many metres to go, until you are close enough; then green, pulsing, "ARREST!". Pressed too early it is a dive at air (as before). A quick tap anywhere also arrests once it is lit; a tap anywhere while he is out of reach does nothing (no dive by accident). |
| "make it fun. Every tap, anywhere, with either thumb, pulls it towards CUFFED; he drags it back all the time, and harder in a **surge** (the bar shakes red, "HE'S FIGHTING! TAP FASTER!"). Fill it: cuffed, case won. Let it empty: he is up and running, a life is lost, the chase is on again. |
| "when you arrest someone, the graphics are kind of messed up" | seen (picture in the review file): he lay in a T with his bag over his head like a black box, two gloves floated in the air, and your officer was not in the picture at all | your officer **kneels astride him**, both hands on his wrists; he is face down with his wrists crossed behind his back, kicking and rolling as hard as he is fighting; the bag is off; the officer leans in on every tap; the camera is in front of the two of them, with a lamp on them; the cuffs go on his wrists. |

## Research (I cannot play other games; I studied how they are written about)
- **Corners in a runner are a sideways swipe, made a little early.** Temple Run's own scheme, unchanged in its sequel: swipe left or right
  to turn, up to jump, down to slide (Pocket Gamer's Temple Run 2 guide; Gamezebo's walkthrough). Its guide's advice is to swipe "a
  split-second before" the turn rather than at the last moment (Pocket Gamer, Temple Run tips). So ours opens the turn 13 m (about a
  second and a half) before the corner, and the swipe is remembered until you get there.
- **A turn must not be mistaken for another move.** Kotaku's piece on Temple Run liked the controls and was "frustrated by misread
  inputs": a side swipe read as a jump. Temple Run keeps lanes on tilt so a swipe is only ever a turn; we have lanes on swipes, so near a
  corner a sideways swipe is **only** the turn and there is nothing to dodge within 17 m before a corner or 9 m after it.
- **Junctions make a run feel like a chase.** TouchArcade's review: the "twists, turns and crossroads" make it feel "like more of an
  adventure"; Wikipedia: intersections let the player choose a path. Ours are T-junctions where the choice is right or wrong: he went one way.
- **Mashing puts you in the struggle, and must stay short and possible.** GameZone on the button-mashing kind: it has a "solid
  justification", putting the player in the character's shoes in a test of strength; CGMagazine describes one so demanding that no
  trick got the writer past it; the Game Accessibility Guidelines record that mashing "can be seriously painful". So: the struggle is a few
  seconds, two thumbs count (every finger that lands is a tap), nothing is asked that an ordinary thumb cannot do (a clumsy robot at
  under five taps a second wins the first cases; the hardest needs about six), and what he drags back grows slowly with the cases.
- Sources: pocketgamer.com (Temple Run 2 hints and tips; Temple Run hints and tips), gamezebo.com (Temple Run walkthrough),
  kotaku.com ("Temple Run hauls...", 2012), toucharcade.com (Temple Run review), wikipedia.org (Temple Run series),
  gamezone.com (kinds of quick-time event), cgmagonline.com (on a mash nobody could pass), gameaccessibilityguidelines.com (button mashing).

## Rules (part2.js)
- `footPath` lays the run: corners every 41 to 83 m (closer on later cases), only ever heading north, east or west so it never crosses
  itself; `footAt(F, s)` is where the run is s metres along, rounding each corner on a 2.2 m arc.
- Near a corner (13 m) the first sideways swipe is the turn: right way, `cornered`; wrong way or none, `wall` (a stumble of 0.9 s; 1.3 s
  for the wrong way at a T).
- `tap()` on foot is the ARREST button: within `cfg.tackle` it starts the struggle, further it is a dive (0.9 s, he gains 3 m).
- The struggle: bar `m` starts at 0.35; a tap adds 0.055; he takes `cfg.resist` a second (0.11 on case 1 towards 0.2), times 1.7 for the
  0.8 s of a surge, which comes every 1.6 to 2.6 s. The first 0.6 s nothing drains (time to find the button). `m >= 1`: cuffed. `m <= 0`: free.
- Lessons: case 2 teaches the corner (two corners, nothing in the way), then dodging (a straight alley), then the ARREST button; case 3 the struggle.

## His rules, kept
Large controls that never cover the action (the button sits under your officer's feet; the bar above the two of you); something to do in
the first seconds; mistakes explained on the screen ("MISSED THE TURN!", "WRONG WAY! HE WENT LEFT", "TOO FAR!" with what to do);
difficulty from more to read (T-junctions, surges), not only speed; Music, Sound effects and Vibration separate; tutorial optional.
