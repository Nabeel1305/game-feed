# Rex 0.2.0: a stick in one place, look round by dragging, a real T-Rex (23 Nov 2026)

His words (17:08): "you still haven't fixed Rex. Number one, how about you make the joystick in one place, not everywhere I tap...
make it that I can be able to see around the area just by clicking the screen and moving around. And also, you still haven't fixed
the joystick problem... figure out what's wrong... That T-Rex looks like shit... Make him look like an actual T-Rex. Google photos of
T-Rexes."

## What was wrong with the stick (found with real finger touches, not a mouse)
0.1.5 made the stick say a direction on the screen, but the view still drifted while the thumb was down: the camera's position
trailed the rex and its aim was ahead of him, so as he walked sideways the view turned by up to 30 degrees, and "up" walked him
30 degrees off. On letting go the view swung round behind him, so the next push meant something else again. Measured with touch
events sent the way a phone sends them (`Input.dispatchTouchEvent`), not with a mouse, as before.

## The controls now
- **The stick never moves:** bottom left, drawn all the time (a ring, four marks, a knob). It works only where it is drawn (a touch
  within 1.75 of its radius); the knob follows the thumb within the ring. A gentle push turns him to look, further walks, all the
  way charges.
- **Drag anywhere else to look round:** left and right turns the view round him, up and down tilts it (from low to high). Both
  thumbs at once work: walk with one, look with the other.
- **The view never turns by itself.** Camera and aim are both set from one followed point along one heading, so the view cannot
  drift; the stick's up is always the screen's up, whichever way he faces.
- BITE and ROAR stay where they were.
- Checked by `test_touch.py` with the drawn stick: left, right, down, up and up-left each face within 10 degrees and walk within 25
  of the push, with the view still; a touch away from the stick does not walk him; dragging the screen right turns the view right.

## Research: what a T-Rex looks like
From the skeletons and the life restorations (Wikipedia and GBIF on *Tyrannosaurus*; Hutchinson et al. 2011, PLoS ONE, on its
body; the 2018 Saurian restoration; skin studies of tyrannosaurids):
- About 12 m long, 3.7 m at the hip; a massive skull (up to 1.5 m) balanced by a long, heavy tail; the back held level.
- A short, muscular, S-curved neck; a deep, broad skull widest at the cheeks, eyes set to look forward, rough bosses over the eyes.
- Small but strong arms with two clawed fingers; huge thighs; long shins and bird-like feet with three toes forward.
- Small pebbly scales over most of the body, flat scales on the face; colour is not known (countershaded here: dark back, pale belly,
  faint bands across the back).

## The model (`part25_trex.js`)
Every part is one smooth skin swept along a curve (oval cross-sections, Catmull-Rom between them): trunk, tail in two parts (it
sways), neck, skull, lower jaw (drops for the bite, the mouth's inside only seen then), brows and brow horns, eyes with lids and
slit pupils, nostrils, teeth along both jaws, the two-fingered arms, drumstick thighs, knees and ankles that fold as each foot comes
forward, three clawed toes. Skin: a cell pattern (each scale a little dome) for colour and for the bumps (a normal map). The rival is
the same animal, red-brown. The other animals are as they were.
