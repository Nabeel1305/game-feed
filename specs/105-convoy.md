# 105. Convoy Escort (his idea 34, 21 Nov 2026)

## The idea (his words, then mine)
His: command a naval escort protecting a convoy and hunting submarines, with the tension of searching a vast ocean,
reading uncertain signals and deciding when to attack or stay close to the ships you protect.
Mine: one mission is a few minutes: merchant ships steam towards the harbour, submarines wait ahead and to the sides. You
hear them before you find them, ping until you are sure, and either chase and depth-charge or stay close enough that they
will not dare fire. Intensity 3, two thumbs, played sideways. Ships and enemies are original and unnamed (no real navies).

**3D:** yes: the rolling sea, the weather and the ships around you are the tension; a tactical map button shows it flat.

## How it plays (two thumbs, sideways)
- **The wheel:** a floating stick under the left thumb, left or right; the ship turns with weight, and a dashed line shows
  the course for the next half minute. **Throttle** buttons on the left: REVERSE, STOP, CRUISE, FULL.
- **PING** (right, big): the sonar answers with a ring on the sea where a submarine may be (not right astern). Ping again and
  the ring tightens; under 45 m it is SURE (red). Rings grow as they age (orange, with their age in seconds): it has moved.
  **Hydrophones** report propeller noise and a bearing before you are close: a yellow HEARD ring, so you never hunt blind.
- **DROP** with **SHALLOW / MEDIUM / DEEP** above it: a pattern goes off two seconds behind the stern. The reports tell you
  the depth: a periscope or one that just fired is shallow, one that has just dived is deep, otherwise medium. Within 48 m
  at the right depth is a hit (one hit makes it surface; a near miss "felt that").
- **GUN:** a surfaced submarine can be shelled (closer is surer).
- **Deterrence:** a submarine will not fire with an escort within ~500 m: it goes deep and runs, then comes back later.
  Chasing one far away leaves a gap; the next may be coming from the other side.
- **Crew** (top left): two teams for REPAIR (engine, sonar, gun, flooding), FIRE and RESCUE (survivors in rafts, close by).
  Each job takes time; you choose which first.
- **Damage:** a torpedo hit costs hull, starts a fire and flooding, and knocks out the engine, sonar or gun (shown).
- **⏩** fast-forwards the quiet stretches and drops back the moment there is danger; **🗺 MAP** shows convoy, contacts with
  their ages, torpedoes and the harbour.

## Missions and progression
Escort (open water), night runs (no periscopes, faint wakes), rescues (a ship already down: rafts to reach), and a crippled
ship limping home. Weather: morning, grey, squalls, night, storm (shorter sonar). More merchants (2 to 6), two submarines from
level 3, other escorts join you from level 8 (they guard the far side), merchants take two hits from level 7.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves: `s` the wheel anywhere, `c` wheel centred, `t` any throttle, `p` ping, `d` depth charges at any depth,
  `g` gun, `w` a crew job, `f` fast-forward.
- Why they lose: a submarine only holds fire when an escort is close to it, and the ships are torpedoed with a good lead
  (two salvos at two ships); charges only hit within 48 m at the right depth.
- Proved: 0 of 1,408 blind strings, 0 of 120 random.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1. **FIND THE SUBMARINE:** steer to the marker, ping until the ring is sure, then steer right over it; twice.
2. **DEPTH CHARGES:** find him, run over him, drop at MEDIUM; two hits.
3. **GUARD THE CONVOY:** get close to a submarine closing on a merchant before it fires; scare it off twice.

## Research (I cannot play other games; I read about them)
- **Destroyer: The U-Boat Hunter** (Subsim, Steam, GOG): protecting convoys is rewarding because there is always something
  to do: depth charges, sonar, plotting a course, directing other escorts
  (https://www.subsim.com/get-out-of-my-convoy-u-boat). So: sonar, depth settings, the course line, other escorts at higher
  levels, all on one screen with big buttons.
- **Atlantic Fleet** (Subsim): subs tracked by sonar, depth-charge attacks, torpedoes only from periscope depth; a known bug
  let destroyers shell submarines at periscope depth (https://www.subsim.com/ssr/atlantic_fleet/review_atlantic_fleet.php).
  So: a submarine that fires is shallow (that is the depth clue), and the gun works only on surfaced ones.
- **Silent Hunter III** (GameSpot): fighting on the surface near destroyers is suicide
  (https://www.gamespot.com/videos/silent-hunter-iii-video-review/2300-6121287/). So: a damaged submarine surfaces and the
  gun finishes it; submarines dive away from a close escort.
- **Silent Hunter mobile** (Pocket Gamer): atmosphere and sound were good but laughably easy enemies made it dull
  (https://www.pocketgamer.com/silent-hunter/review/). So: submarines that lead their torpedoes well and come back after
  evading; the convoy is genuinely at risk.
- **Crash Dive** (App Store): a sub game designed for touch screens, blending simulation with arcade action
  (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/crash-dive/id884516166). So: simplified depth bands and contacts, real tactics underneath.
- **UBOAT** (Subsim): its damage and repair model shines (https://www.subsim.com/ssr/uboat/review_uboat.php). So: hits knock out
  specific systems and the crew repairs them one at a time.

## His directions, point by point
- View: 3D ocean, weather and light by mission; elevated view; tactical map button. Kept.
- Movement: left steering, throttle with reverse/stop/cruise/full, heavy turns, projected course. Kept.
- Action: big right buttons for sonar, depth (weapon setting) and firing; routine crew tasks automated (pumps, reloads). Kept.
- Basic mission: escort through the area, investigate contacts, intercept, keep enough ships safe. Kept.
- First mission: your ship, two merchants, one submarine; lessons for steering, sonar, interception. Kept.
- Finding enemies: hidden until detected; approximate first, tighter with repeats; sure vs old. Kept.
- Readable information: contact age shown, rings grow with time; hydrophone bearings so nothing is guesswork. Kept.
- Attacks: crew reports, periscopes (day), torpedo wakes (faint at night), time to react. Kept.
- Weapons: deck gun for surfaced, depth charges with a predicted area and shallow/medium/deep. Kept (the drop point is behind
  the stern, shown when a sure ring is under you).
- Decisions: chasing leaves a gap; two submarines from two sides. Kept.
- Defence: being near the threatened ship stops the attack; turning and position matter more than firing. Kept.
- Crew: repair, fight fire, rescue survivors; two teams. Kept.
- Damage: flooding, fire, reduced speed, broken equipment, explained; recover with repairs. Kept.
- Variety: escorts, night, rescue, a damaged vessel. Aircraft and surface ships: later (as he says, after submarines feel
  complete). Kept.
- Pacing: quiet and sudden; fast-forward that stops for danger. Kept.
- Sound: hull and waves, engines, pings, distant explosions, crew reports; restrained music that builds. Kept.
- Vibration and shake: firing, explosions, hits; both switchable. Kept.
- Fair difficulty: consistent rules for detection and attack; more ships, more submarines, weather. Kept.
- Success: convoy, ship, rescues and kills scored; short missions; one tap to retry. Kept.

## Engagement factors chosen (PLAYBOOK)
P4 fair, readable failure (every hit says what broke; every report says why), P6 every input answers back (ping and echo,
rings, wakes), P14 goal gradient (the distance to the harbour counting down), P15 nested goals (each contact, each ship,
the harbour, three stars). Sound rules S1-S11. Longevity L1-L10: steer and ping in the first seconds; wheel, throttle and
three buttons; rules you can learn (they never fire near you); the harbour distance always on top; score and stars;
missions, weather and more escorts later; chase or guard; TRY AGAIN at once; paint by stars and dockyard upgrades; best per
level.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
A sure ring under the bow, DROP, and the columns of white water going up behind the stern, then "SUBMARINE SURFACING!".
