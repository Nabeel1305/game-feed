# Audit of games 9-111 (22 Nov 2026)
His words: "lots of them are below average... go through these games and play them... figure out the problems yourself and fix
them." Games 1-8 (on the app) are left alone. How I looked: each game played by its own robot on a phone-sized screen with the
real 3D picture, level 1's first seconds and level 5 at 3, 10 and 20 s (`/tmp/audit/shoot.py`), plus its sounds and code.
C = critical (nearly unplayable or ugly enough to put him off), F = flaw.

## Seen across many games
- **Dark levels**: the night, dusk, storm and mist skies leave the play area nearly black or washed out (Vine, Pilot, Wild
  Water, Hunter, Police Chase). Fix in `tools/world3d.js`: a floor on brightness, lighter fog, then rebuild every 3D game.
- **Robotic music**: every tune is one plucked line on a fixed grid. Fix in `tools/juice-kit.js`: a small band (drums, bass,
  chords, lead) with swing, humanised timing, reverb, and a style per game.

## His note, 22 Nov 2026, 12:19 (while I was working)
"The controls on that hunting game... are awful. The way you shoot, there's supposed to be a circle, or three lines (a crosshair)
that show you where to aim, and you're supposed to move it with one hand, and creep with the other. Maybe if you go slowly, you
creep. If you go faster, you run... check the rest of the games and make sure they actually have good, easy controls. The minute
someone opens this game, they don't need to be a genius to understand the controls. Because you're a genius, and that's what you
forget."
- **Hunter (#13): rebuild the controls**: left thumb = a floating stick (gentle = creep, full = run), right thumb = drag a
  crosshair, let go (or a FIRE button) to throw/shoot.
- **Controls check for every game**: can a first-time player tell what to do in three seconds from what is on screen (a
  labelled stick, a labelled button, a finger hint)? Anything with hidden gestures, swipes with meaning, or more than a stick
  and two buttons gets simplified.
