# 17. Country Vet

## The idea (his words, then mine)
His list (17 Nov 2026): "you're a vet doctor. So animals come to you. Maybe this thing, a cow comes to you and you have to take
care of its hooves... A bunch of animals come to you with problems, and you have to take care of them." Calm (2), one thumb.
Inside an old wooden barn in 3D: plank walls, straw on the floor, a lamp on a beam, the big door open onto the farmyard with
the level's weather and light. Dogs, cows, sheep, horses, pigs and goats walk in one at a time, each with a name.

## How it plays (one thumb, each tool used the way it is really used)
- The card says what the owner noticed: Limping, Filthy, Scratched, Off her food, Shaking her head. You look at her to see what
  and where: a thorn in a paw, a hoof grown too long, a cut on a leg, caked mud, a red ear; the sore leg is held up.
- The tray at the bottom: Hand, Tweezers, Rasp, Spray, Wrap, Needle, Brush, Drops. Put the tool on the right part of her and the
  camera moves in close:
  - Tweezers on the thorn, then pull away **slowly** (too fast hurts and it slips back).
  - Rasp: strokes along the hoof down to the line; short ones near the end (past the line is sore).
  - Spray: hold it on the cut until clean. Wrap: circle round the leg, unhurried (too fast is too tight).
  - Needle: hold it on her neck; when she shivers, lift off at once, then go back in.
  - Brush: strokes with the hair, head to tail (against it pulls).
  - Drops: a tap on the ear each time her head is still.
- The calm bar on the card: rough handling, the wrong tool, poking where nothing is wrong all frighten her. With your hand,
  stroke her slowly along her back to settle her (a quick flick startles her). Past the mark she will not hold still; full, she
  bolts out of the barn. Three bolt and the day is over.

## Levels: very easy first, then harder for ever (LESSONS rule 0)
Level 1: two dogs with a thorn, calm, a wide pull. Then more animals (horses are the most nervous), more patients a day (up to
5, 6 on every tenth), two problems each from level 5, animals coming in more frightened, fear rising by itself while it hurts
(from level 4), a slower pull, a finer line on the hoof, slower wraps, a quicker shiver. Help after three losses gives time.
Robots: an ordinary player wins all of 1 to 20; a sloppy one 1 to 10; a sharp one 7 in 10 of level 40.

## Spamming cannot win (LESSONS rule 1)
- Blind moves (BLIND in part2): a gentle stroke, any tool, a tool put on any part, a gentle pull, a rasp stroke, a turn of
  bandage, a brush stroke either way, a drop, lift off.
- Each wrong tool or poke frightens her more than a stroke calms her, and the problem and where it is change with every animal.
- Every blind string of up to four moves on 8 beats on levels 1, 2, 3 and 6: 64 of 182,976 won (0.035%, under one in a
  thousand). Random moves: 0 of 480.

## Lessons of the first three levels
1: calm her (three slow strokes), then the thorn. 2: trim a cow's hoof to the line; brush a muddy sheep with the hair.
3: clean and wrap a horse's cut; give a pig her injection, lifting off when she shivers. Nothing is lost during a lesson.

## Engagement factors chosen
P6 every input answers back (each tool has its own sound, the animal reacts), P10 sound with a visual twin, P18 gentle visits in
a row (the chime climbs), P20 a gentle palette (a warm barn, golden light), P30 the animals' own voices and a happy call when
better. No clock: fear rises slowly only on later levels (P19 in spirit). Sounds from the kit: birdsong, rain on the roof.

## The card clip: its most exciting moment
A horse with a thorn: the tweezers go on, the slow pull, the thorn pops out, "STAR FEELS BETTER" and a happy whinny.
